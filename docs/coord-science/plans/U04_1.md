# C4.1 Electrolysis — lesson & unit plan (COORD_SCI / U04_1)

The first unit of the **IGCSE Coordinated Science** track (`COORD_SCI`), adapted
from the classroom deck at `C:\Users\bowen\lessons\content\coord-science\U04_1`.
This track is **English-only** (`bilingual: false` in the track registry) — the
IGCSE exam language — so there are no `vn*` twins.

## Objective

The student can define electrolysis and an electrolyte, explain why a compound
must be molten or dissolved, label an electrolytic cell, read the molten lead(II)
bromide equation symbol by symbol, write and add both half-equations, predict the
products of any molten binary salt, **write balanced symbol equations from word
equations**, and reason about the electrolysis of copper(II) sulfate solution.

## The Formulae task — name → formula (`FORMULA_WRITE`)

The step BEFORE writing equations. For each compound the student sets each ion's
**charge** (the superscript, e.g. Pb²⁺, Br⁻) and the **subscripts** that make the
compound neutral (PbBr₂). A live charge-balance meter shows positive vs negative
charge, so the crossover rule is something they see, not memorise. Component:
`src/tasks/FormulaWrite.jsx`; shared chem rendering (sub/superscripts) in
`src/tasks/chemWidgets.jsx`. Both this and the Equations task were polished to read
as one system.

The Workbook uses only answerable widgets (multiple choice, dropdowns, drag-to-
electrode) — no free-typed equations, which the Equations task now covers. The
later Notes discussion questions are `check` MCQs (choose an answer, no spoiler),
not reveal panels.

## The new task — Symbol Equations (`SYMBOL_EQ`)

"Turn the word equation into a balanced symbol equation." For each reactant and
product the student picks a formula from a candidate bank and sets a coefficient;
`src/utils/chemFormula.js` parses each chosen formula into atom counts and derives
whether the equation both uses the right species **and** balances. Nothing is
compared against a stored answer string — a wrong key cannot ship (every authored
item is checked by `chemFormula.checkItem`). Component: `src/tasks/SymbolEquation.jsx`.

Items (all molten binary salts splitting into elements): PbBr₂, 2NaCl→2Na+Cl₂,
2KI→2K+I₂, ZnCl₂, CuCl₂, 2Al₂O₃→4Al+3O₂.

## Key words (13 in the notebook; 11 in Vocab)

electrolyte · non-electrolyte · electrolysis · electrode · inert · cathode ·
anode · cation · anion · reduction · oxidation · binary substance · half-equation.

## Equations the student meets

- Overall: `PbBr₂(l) → Pb(l) + Br₂(g)`
- Cathode: `Pb²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Pb` (reduction)
- Anode: `2Br⁻ → Br₂ + 2e⁻` (oxidation)

## The three discussion questions (Short Answers / Notes reveals)

1. Why must the substance be molten or dissolved? — ions locked in a solid lattice
   are free to move only when molten/dissolved.
2. Electrolysis of brine — chlorine at the anode, hydrogen (from water, not sodium)
   at the cathode, sodium hydroxide left in solution.
3. Economic importance — extracting reactive metals (Al, Na), chlorine + NaOH from
   brine, electroplating, hydrogen fuel cells.
