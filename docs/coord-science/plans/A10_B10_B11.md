# Assignment 10 — Coordination, Response and Reproduction (COORD_SCI / B10_1, B11_1, B11_2)

Three units built on 2026-09-30 from two things the teacher supplied:

- **the homework** — Wolsey Hall IGCSE Co-ordinated Sciences **Assignment 10**
  ("Coordination, Response and Reproduction", 70 marks: 20 multiple choice and
  structured questions 21–25);
- **38 snips of the coursebook** — Cambridge IGCSE Co-ordinated Sciences,
  chapters **B10** Coordination and response and **B11** Reproduction.

The assignment covers **both** chapters, so it became three units rather than
one: a student asked to do all of it in one sitting would face a 60-slide deck.

| Unit | Coursebook | Assignment 10 questions reviewed |
|---|---|---|
| `B10_1` Coordination and Response | B10.01 nervous system · B10.02 hormones · B10.03 homeostasis | 1–10, 22, 25 (26 marks) |
| `B11_1` Reproduction in Plants | B11.01 asexual and sexual · B11.02 plants | 11, 13, 14, 16, 19, 20, 21 (16 marks) |
| `B11_2` Reproduction in Humans | B11.03 humans · B11.04 STIs | 12, 15, 17, 18, 23, 24 (28 marks) |

Unit ids keep the coursebook's letter (`B10_1` shows as "B10.1"); the track's
registry entry now has two sections, **Biology** (`B…`) and **Chemistry** (the
older `U04_1`, `U05_1`, whose ids cannot change — progress is keyed on them).

## What the teacher asked for

**1. Notes that are clearer, with much more to write down and to draw.**
Each deck is written to be copied into a notebook:

- an orange **Write This Down** card on nearly every slide, in lines short
  enough to copy in one go (definitions are the syllabus wording);
- a yellow **Draw This** corner on the diagrams — 9, 7 and 9 of them;
- "copy these as a table" slides for the part-and-function lists (glands and
  hormones, the parts of a flower, the male and female systems);
- a **Watch Out** slide for the words that cost marks (glucose / glycogen /
  glucagon · pollination / fertilisation · urethra / ureter / uterus);
- a closing checklist that says how many definitions and diagrams the notebook
  should now hold.

Body copy is two or three short sentences a slide. Beside a check, a split
slide's text column is about 330 px wide on a 1280 × 720 laptop, and the first
draft's paragraphs pushed the orange card off the bottom of it.

**2. One task that goes over the homework itself** — the new **Homework Review**
task (`HW_REVIEW`, dbKey `p60`, 20 XP), below.

## Gate structure (all three units)

| Gate | Threshold | Tasks |

| 0 · Learn | 0 | **Notes** 20 · **Vocab** 10 |
| 1 · Apply | 20 | **Label It** 20 · **Practice** 10 · **Questions** 20 · **Homework Review** 20 |
| 2 · Quiz | 70 | **Quiz** 20 · **Games** 0 |

120 XP on offer, capped at 100. Gate 1 is 20 of 30 (67%), Gate 2 is 70 of 100
(70%) — inside the validator's 80% rule. Notes is worth 20 here (10 in the two
chemistry units) because the decks are long and carry 16 scored items each.

## Homework Review (`HW_REVIEW`)

Every question of the assignment, asked again one at a time and then worked:
**How to get it** (steps) · **Mark scheme** · **Model answer — copy it into your
notebook** · a one-line **tip** on exam technique. A tag above each prompt says
which question it is ("Assignment 10 · Question 22(c)(ii)") and how many marks.

It is the **Workbook screen** with a third data key (`unit.hwReview`), not a new
component, so it resumes and checkpoints exactly as Practice does. What had to
be added to `Workbook.jsx` is listed at the top of that file: `source`, `marks`,
`image`, `wide`, `stack`, `figureNote`, `markScheme`, `modelAnswer`, `tip`, and
a group `theme`. A question with a figure now shows it **beside** the question
from `lg` up, so the options are not pushed off a laptop screen.

**Written questions are re-asked in a form the screen can mark.** The paper's
"Explain the results between 20–30 minutes (3 marks)" becomes six statements to
sort into *earns a mark* / *does not earn a mark*; "name the neurone labelled A"
becomes a typed word; the letter tables become dropdowns. That makes the mark
scheme the thing being practised, and needs no AI marking.

The validator checks `hwReview` like a quiz: every question has a `source`,
solution steps and a markable answer, and every drag chip has a home.

## Label It takes pictures

`LABEL_IT` could only strip the labels off an authored SVG. An item may now be a
**picture** (`image`, with `viewBox` = its pixel size) whose labels have been
painted out, a blank box going on the end of each printed leader line. Seven of
the ten diagrams across the three units are coursebook figures used this way;
the pin positions are printed by the build script below.

## Figures and the build script

`docs/coord-science/tools/build_a10_figures.py <snips folder> <assignment pdf>`
crops every figure from the snips into `public/images/COORD_SCI/<unit>/`, makes
the `*-blank` variants (labels painted out) for Label It and the `hw-*` variants
(letters in place of labels) for the review, and prints each pin. The snips and
the PDF are **not** in the repo.

Drawn rather than cropped (`diagrams.js` in each unit): the response chain, the
glucose loop, negative feedback, the two glucose graphs, the reflex with
neurones A–E; chromosome numbers, the wind-pollinated flower, the pollen tube,
two kinds of pollen, germination, the four flasks; the sperm and egg cells, the
28-day wheel, a bacterium and a virus, the HIV graphs.

**Copyright.** The cropped figures are from a copyrighted coursebook and are
used at the teacher's direction, for his own students who own the book. They are
listed in `docs/credits.md` under their own heading. The two photographs that
were cropped (a bee, a pollen micrograph) were **not** published: a CC
photograph from Wikimedia Commons would do the same job and can be added.

## Two facts worth knowing

- The coursebook's chapter summary prints ovulation at "about day 11". The
  assignment (Q15: days 13–16) and the syllabus use **day 14**, and so do these
  units.
- **Wind pollination, the pollen tube and germination** appear in the snips only
  as lines of the chapter summary, and **implantation and meiosis** not at all.
  Those slides are written from the summary and the syllabus, because the
  assignment asks about every one of them.
