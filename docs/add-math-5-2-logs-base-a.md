# Add Maths 5.2 — Logarithms to base a

Source section: Cambridge IGCSE and O Level Additional Mathematics coursebook (3rd
edition), section 5.2, pages 104–106. Read in the Cambridge GO reader on 2026-09-30.

Three parts: the beats of the notes in our own words, a map of the exercise's question
types, and a fresh problem set that covers every type, with answers. None of the
problems are the book's. Every answer was checked by script.

## Part 1 — The beats of the notes

1. **Same idea, any base (p. 104).** If y = aˣ then x = logₐ y.
2. **The four facts again.** logₐ a = 1, logₐ 1 = 0, logₐ aˣ = x, and a^(logₐ x) = x.
3. **The picture.** y = aˣ and y = logₐ x are reflections in y = x.
4. **When a log exists.** The base must be positive and not 1. The number you take the
   log of must be positive.
5. **Worked example 4.** Exponential form to log form, by the same two methods as 5.1
   (the three-step frame, or taking logs of both sides).
6. **Worked example 5 (p. 105).** Log form to exponential form, two methods.
7. **Worked example 6.** Evaluate by writing the number as a power of the base: a whole
   power, a larger whole power, and a fraction giving a negative answer.

## Part 2 — What the exercise asks

Exercise 5.2 is on pages 105–106: six questions.

| Type | Book question | What it asks | How it climbs |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Exponential form to log form | 1 (nine parts) | Rewrite the statement | Whole powers, negative powers giving fractions, then letters |
| B. Log form to exponential form | 2 (nine parts) | Rewrite the statement | Whole answers, zero, fractional answers, then letters |
| C. Solve simple log equations | 3 (nine parts) | Find x | x as the number, x as the base, then a linear expression inside the log |
| D. Find the value | 4 (twelve parts) | Evaluate without a calculator | Whole powers, decimals, surds, reciprocals of surds, a base that is a power of the answer |
| E. Simplify logₓ of a power of x | 5 (eight parts) | Evaluate | Powers, roots, reciprocals, then products and quotients of them |
| F. Nested logs | 6 (two parts) | Solve a log of a log | Work from the outside in |

## Answers

**A.** A1 log₇ 343 = 3. A2 log₂ 1024 = 10. A3 log₉ 81 = 2. A4 log₅ 625 = 4.
A5 log₂ (1/8) = −3. A6 log₆ (1/36) = −2. A7 log₂₅ 5 = ½. A8 log_p q = 3. A9 log_m 7 = n.

**B.** B1 2³ = 8. B2 3³ = 27. B3 7⁰ = 1. B4 6¹ = 6. B5 49^½ = 7. B6 27^⅓ = 3.
B7 4⁻³ = 1/64. B8 tʷ = 5. B9 r² = s.

**C.** C1 32. C2 64. C3 1. C4 4. C5 ½. C6 9 (−9 is rejected: a base is positive).
C7 5. C8 49. C9 25. C10 11. C11 −4.5 (the log is of 16, so this is allowed).
C12 3 or −3 (both give a log of 5).

**D.** D1 8. D2 3. D3 ½. D4 −1. D5 −3. D6 5/2. D7 7/3. D8 −½. D9 −5/2. D10 1/5.
D11 2/3. D12 3/2. D13 −5/3. D14 6.

**E.** E1 5. E2 ½. E3 5/2. E4 −3. E5 −½. E6 6. E7 7/4. E8 5/6.

**F.** F1 log₃ x = 2, so x = 9. F2 log₂ x = 9, so x = 512. F3 log₂ x = 2, so x = 4.
F4 log₂ (log₃ x) = 1, log₃ x = 2, x = 9.

**G.** G1 x > 3. G2 Every power of 1 is 1, so 1ˣ = y has no solution unless y = 1, and
then every x works. G3 7, 5, and 14. G4 x² = 2x + 3 gives x = 3 or −1; −1 is rejected
because a base must be positive. x = 3.

## Notes for building

- Already covered by `AM_5A`; the Log Simplifier's "evaluate" items are type D.
- Type C mixes three different unknowns (the number, the base, a bracket). The book puts
  them in one question in that order. A task should keep that order.
- C11 and C12 are deliberate: a negative answer that is valid, and two roots that are
  both valid. They stop "reject the negative one" becoming a reflex before 5.4.
