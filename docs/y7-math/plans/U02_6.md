# U02_6 · 2.6 Inequalities (self-study)

The independent-learning adaptation of the classroom deck
(`C:\Users\bowen\lessons`, `content/y7-math/U02_6`, 28 slides), built to the
number-engines unit shape in [../number-engines.md](../number-engines.md) and the
deck density in [../algebra-engines.md](../algebra-engines.md) §4.

**Objective:** I can say "is less than" and "is greater than" in English, reading
left to right, and recognise the other words a word problem uses for them; say
what an inequality and an integer are; show an inequality on a number line with an
open circle and an arrow, and read one back; and find the smallest or largest
integer that works, even below zero.
**Key words:** inequality, integer, less than, greater than, open circle, fewer —
the book's two margin words for 2.6, the two phrases the signs are read as, the
picture's own word, and one of the word-problem words.
**Classroom twin:** `meta.classroom` → `y7-math/U02_6`. The classroom lesson's
`meta.dashboard` back-link (`{ track: 'Y7_MATH', unit: 'U02_6' }`) is not set by
this build — it lives in the other repo.

## The three things that shape the deck (kept from the classroom)
- **The English.** Slides 3–4 and 8–9. The signs are Grade 1 in Vietnam; what is new
  is "is less than" / "is greater than" read left to right, and fewer, below,
  under, more, above, over. The WORDS slide survives as a `sort` of six sentences.
- **Many answers.** Slides 5–7: the cats as a `predict`, inequality against
  equation, and integer — the check asks which of 3, 3.5, 2 and 12 an integer
  c > 3 could be.
- **The two mistakes.** Each hand-vote is a `predict` with no answer on its slide:
  x > 3 (3 or 4), settled by the open circle on 13; t < −2, settled by "less than
  means left" on 17 and the thermometer on 18.

## Narration (the audio reads the signs)
`generate_all_audio.py` reads `$x > 3$` as "x is greater than 3" and a lone `$<$`
as "less than". The deck is written for that:
- no bare or bold sign in a narrated field (a bare `<` is left as a symbol and a
  bare `>` is deleted);
- a negative number straight after a sign is written with a keyboard minus inside
  the maths (`$t < -2$`) — the generator does not read a sign followed by `−`;
- an inequality the student has to SAY (slides 3 and 9) sits in the split's
  `example` box, which a layout slide's narration skips; read from `content`, the
  audio would say "k is less than −1", which is slide 9's answer.
Reads awkwardly, and left: slide 3's note, "less than means is less than. greater
than means is greater than."

## Cut / changed from the classroom deck
- **Starter** (write < or > in three boxes) → slide 2, an `order` of the same six
  numbers. Not a `sort` into < and >: the next activity (slide 4) is a sort.
- **Classroom 2 (say it) + 3 (the Remember box)** fold into slide 3: the three
  pairs are shown with their sentences, as the model for slide 9.
- **Votes → `predict`** (slides 12 and 16). The ANS_3 / ANS_4 / ANS_UP / ANS_DOWN
  raised-hand cards are not ported.
- **Whiteboards / out-loud slides → one scored item, the rest in a reveal**
  (8, 9, 11, 15, 21). On 21 the scored one is q < −7, not the classroom's q < −4,
  which is the exit question's number; e is w > 4.5, not p > 2.5, which the
  stepper shows on the slide before.
- **Read the Line comes before the vote** (classroom 14 → 11), straight after the
  picture is taught, so the two `ineq` activities (read, draw) are not back to
  back. READ_LINES loses its fourth line, which is the activity.
- **Slide 13 gains a `hotspot`** (new diagram HOT_LINE): the vote is settled, then
  the student taps the smallest integer for m > 9 — the open circle is a decoy.
- **Could It Be?** (a room game) is not ported as a widget. Six of its cards are a
  `sort` on slide 19, moved AFTER the negatives so they can carry both mistakes
  without spoiling the vote on 16. Quick Fire's `couldbe` mode deals the rest.
- **The thermometer** is the slide's `image`, upright; the classroom's THERMO_REAL
  embedded the photo twice in an SVG (upright and rotated) by import. The text
  does the turning, and says the scale prints no minus signs.
- **Mr Bowen's Homework** keeps the drawing and becomes a `sort` by slip. b and d
  are the same slip in opposite directions, so there are four bins.
- **Word problems**: the class is an `ineq` list with the two inequalities given;
  the cats (0) and Mr Bowen's number (none) are checks, and the student turns the
  sentences into inequalities without being shown them.
- **EQ_VS_INEQ** says "last lesson" and "this lesson" (classroom: "2.5" and
  "today"). Decimals are written with a point in both languages.
- **Cut:** the homework-assignment slide, the "Lesson Complete!" exit hero (its
  question is the checklist's check), the date, the "6 written panels" count, the
  Could It Be? widget, and every whiteboard / pairs / hands instruction.
