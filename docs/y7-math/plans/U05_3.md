# U05_3 · 5.3 Drawing lines and quadrilaterals (self-study)

> **Status: plan approved for Phase B (with the coordinator's notes folded in).**

The self-study version of the classroom deck `y7-math/U05_3` (25 slides, one period,
EN/FR). It is a practical lesson: ruler, protractor, sharp pencil, set square. The
unit's own engine is **Zero on the Line** (`ZERO_LINE`, p83). **English and French
only** (`meta.vietnamese: false`), `meta.icon: 'TriangleRight'` (it is in the IconMap).

**Objective:** read a protractor (estimate first, then count up from the 0 on the
line); draw an angle; know a square needs right angles as well as equal sides; draw the
perpendicular from a point and a parallel line (the same angle); draw a quadrilateral
from a sketch; measure to the nearest mm and degree; check a drawing with 360°.
**Classroom twin:** `meta.classroom` → `y7-math/U05_3`. The back-link is for integration.

## The tension: real tools, a student alone with a screen

The skill is a real protractor on real paper, but the mistakes this section is about
are **decisions**, not hand movements. Which 0 sits on the line? Count up from it.
Does the reading agree with my estimate? At B, the angle is measured from BA, so it is
the other 0. Copy the *same* angle at P, not 180° − it. Do the four angles add up to
360°? In the room, Mr Bowen walks round and sees "115°" where 65° was asked for. On
paper at home nobody sees it. On screen every one of those decisions can be made,
marked and **named**. So the flagship is on screen, and it tests decisions, not
dexterity: the student never rotates or drags a protractor by finger.

The hand skill still needs to be practised once, and this section makes that honest.
Its answers **check themselves**: a 6 cm square's diagonals are both ≈ 8.5 cm, and the
angles C and D of a quadrilateral drawn from a sketch need trigonometry to calculate,
which Year 7 students don't have. So when the student types their measurements, the app
can mark a real paper drawing. That is the engine's **last level, "With your own
tools"**. A student without tools can skip it and still finish the task. **Nowhere else
in the unit says "on paper".**

## Zero on the Line — `ZERO_LINE` (p83), unit key `zeroLine`
Engine `src/utils/zeroLine.js` (pure) · screen `src/tasks/ZeroLine.jsx` · deck activity
`zeroline` (`src/components/notes/ZeroLineActivity.jsx`, beside the slide). The
protractor drawing and the board live in the unit folder (`U05_3/protractor.jsx`).

**Decisions, not dexterity.** The protractor is always placed for the student: its
centre on the vertex, its baseline on the line or arm. The student's decisions are:
- **Estimate**: one tap (acute · obtuse · reflex)
- **Zero**: tap the 0 that sits on the line
- **Read**: type the degrees
- **Mark**: tap the scale. The dot snaps to 1°, with − / + nudges and no number shown
- **Place**: tap AB where the protractor's 90° line passes through C
- **Measure**: type a ruler reading

"Look closer" zooms the board around the crossing. It works by touch at 375 px wide and
at 1280×720.

**Two or three actions an item; extra stages only where the slip happened.** Levels 1–2
teach the full read: Estimate → Zero → Read. From level 3 an item is two actions:
- a read is Estimate → Read
- a draw is Zero → Mark
- the perpendicular is Place → Measure
- a paper item is its boxes

An extra stage opens **only after a wrong answer, at the slip**:
- a `scale` or `notzero` read opens *Zero* ("tap the 0 on the arm"), then the read again
- an `inside` reflex answer opens *Subtract* ("the inside is 125°; 360 − 125 = ?")

A wrong answer is named and tried once more. A second wrong, or Show me, fills the stage
in and the item pays ½.

**Slips named:**
- `estimate`: the estimate tap is wrong
- `zero`: the 0 at the far end
- `scale`: 180 − x. It cites the student's own estimate ("you said acute; 145 is obtuse")
- `notzero`: counted from 0 when the arm sits on 20
- `mismatch`: the reading disagrees with the estimate but is not 180 − x
- `ten`: 10° out
- `tick`: 2–8° out ("each small mark is 1°")
- `inside`: the inside angle given for a reflex one
- `notsame`: the line at P is not parallel; it is drawn on, and it meets AB
- `middle`: the perpendicular stopped at the middle of AB
- `fromB`: measured from the wrong end
- `units`: mm typed for cm (a nudge, not a try)
- `corner`: on paper, the diagonals are unequal or far out

**Paper level.** Tolerances are **±0.2 cm** and **±2°** (the Workbook allows ±1 mm and
±1°; a whole construction adds up its errors).
- After Check the student sees **their value beside the expected one**, with the named
  slip.
- An item pays 1 when every box is in tolerance and ½ otherwise. There is no retry,
  since the expected value is shown.
- **Skip** ("no ruler and protractor with me") marks a paper item skipped. Skipped items
  leave the denominator, so the task finishes and pays in full for everything else.
- Blob `{ [id]: 1 | 0.5 | 'skip' }`; score = 10 × earned ÷ (items − skipped).

**Deck `zeroline`** (read, or draw, optionally on a `shape`) follows the same stages. A
wrong answer is named and tried once more; a second wrong fills the stage in and the
item scores wrong.

**Proof.** `checkZeroLineConfig` runs at `@@U05_3_CHECK@@`. Every item's own answer is
accepted at every stage, and each slip's canonical wrong value is named by its own code.
It refuses:
- |180 − 2x| < 14 for reads and draws
- an arm within 12° of the protractor's ends
- an offset other than 20, 30 or 40
- a reflex angle outside 190–340
- a figure that leaves the board, or a measured angle under 25°
- paper slip bands that overlap the accepted band
- a missing Fr twin

A Node run deals random read and draw items to show no slip collisions.
