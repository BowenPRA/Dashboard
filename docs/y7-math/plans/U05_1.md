# U05_1 · 5.1 A sum of 360° (self-study) — DRAFT, awaiting review

The self-study twin of the classroom deck `y7-math/U05_1` (26 slides, one period).
**English and French only** (`meta.vietnamese: false`). One idea runs through it: **find a
missing angle and say why** — which fact (on a line 180°, round a point 360°, in a
quadrilateral 360°), add what you know, subtract from the total, then give the fact as the
reason. The deck meets each fact and predicts before it is told; one engine does the work.

**Objective:** I can read an angle diagram (a number is known, a letter is missing, a small
square is 90°); find a missing angle on a straight line, round a point and in a
quadrilateral in two steps; share what is left between equal angles; use the opposite
angles of a parallelogram; and give the fact as my reason.
**Classroom twin:** `meta.classroom` → `y7-math/U05_1`. Back-link: integrator.

**Stages** (per letter): *Find first* (two-letter items: tap the letter whose fact has only
one unknown) → *Decide* 180° or 360° → *Add* (tap every angle you know on the figure,
small squares included, then type the total; skipped with one known) → *Subtract* →
*Share* (equal letters) → *Say why* (three reason sentences). A parallelogram's opposite
angle is typed, then its reason. `straight`/`full`: Add → Yes/No → Say why. The done line
is the Workbook answer: "a = 180° − 128° = 52°, because angles on a straight line add up to 180°."

**Named slips:** `point180` (180° for a point), `line360`, `quad180`; `twoUnknown` (found
the wrong letter first); `forgotRight` (the small square), `forgot`, `decoy` (an angle
outside this line or shape — the extended side's angle); `noSub` (wrote the total),
`added`, `arith`; `noShare`, `shareAll` (÷ 4 not ÷ 3); `notOpp`; `wrongFact` (round a
point for a quadrilateral), `wrongNumber`, `looks` ("it looks like 50°" / "it looks
straight"). A wrong answer is named and tried again; a second (or Show me) fills the stage
in and the item pays ½. Blob `{ id: 1 | 0.5 }`, out of 10.

**Figure:** drawn **to scale** (unlike the book), rotated by the id, knowns teal, letters
orange; quadrilaterals built from their angles (convex, 40°–150°). 178° looks straight on
purpose. **Proof:** `checkAngleSumItems` (validator marker): whole degrees, totals right,
every stage accepts its own answer, each slip's canonical answer is named by its own code
and collides with nothing, two-step items have one letter findable first, the figure
closes and fits; `checkAngleSumActivity` for the deck; plus a Node fuzz in the report.

**Levels (15 items, fresh numbers):** 1 on a line (2) · 2 round a point, a reflex answer
and a small square (3) · 3 in a quadrilateral, the garden story (3) · 4 the same size (2) ·
5 is it straight / a full turn? (2) · 6 two steps: a side extended both ways, a
parallelogram (3).
