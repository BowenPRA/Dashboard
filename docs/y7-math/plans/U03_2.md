# U03_2 · 3.2 Rounding (self-study)

The independent-learning adaptation of the **second half** of the classroom deck
(`C:\Users\bowen\lessons`, `content/y7-math/U03_1_2` — slide 17, the place-value
table, then slides 19–39), built to the number-engines unit shape in
[../number-engines.md](../number-engines.md) and the deck density in
[../algebra-engines.md](../algebra-engines.md) §4. The first half is
[U03_1](U03_1.md).

**Objective:** I can count decimal places from the point, round using the next
digit and keep a zero at the end when the answer needs one, read "correct to" as
round, and divide the short way — carry each remainder onto the next digit, add
zeros after the point, and work one place further before I round once.
**Key words:** round, decimal places, degree of accuracy, correct to, remainder,
nearest — the section's two margin words (round, degree of accuracy), the one the
counting depends on, the exam's two instruction words, and the word the bus stop
leans on.
**Classroom twin:** `meta.classroom` → `y7-math/U03_1_2` (the same entry as
U03_1: one classroom lesson, two units). The classroom lesson's `meta.dashboard`
back-link is not set by this build — it holds one `{ track, unit }` and the deck
now has two.

## ADDED — short division, the short way (slides 14–18)
The classroom deck never draws a division: it only compares 8.285 with 8.286.
The unit teaches the method the comparison needs, written the short way —
**no stack of products and subtractions under the number**.
- **14 Short Division** (LONG_SHORT, new): 852 ÷ 6 written twice, long and short.
  The short way writes only the remainders.
- **15 The Remainder Rides** (BUS_CARRY, new): the layout, and the carry named —
  each remainder small, up and to the left of the next digit, read with it as one
  number ("read 25"). Check: 744 ÷ 3, which number next? (14).
- **16 Add Zeros After the Point** (BUS_ZEROS, new): 27 ÷ 4 = 6.75 — the digits run
  out, so a point, then zeros, until the remainder is 0; the points line up.
- **17 Until It Stops**: four exact divisions; 7 ÷ 8 is the `busstop` activity.
- **18 58 ÷ 7, the Short Way** (BUS_STOP, new): the vote's division worked to four
  places — carries in orange, the four added zeros in teal — then rounded once.
- **19–20** tie it to the classroom's rule: to round to n places, add zeros until
  there are n + 1 decimal places, then round once (`order`, then `busstop` with
  `dp` on the classroom's own 23 ÷ 9).

## Bus Stop (SHORT_DIV) coverage — 16 items, six levels
L1 carry the remainder: 738 ÷ 6 = 123 · 952 ÷ 7 = 136 · 4608 ÷ 9 = 512.
L2 the point is given: 8.52 ÷ 4 = 2.13 · 17.4 ÷ 6 = 2.9.
L3 add zeros until it stops: 45 ÷ 2 = 22.5 · 5 ÷ 8 = 0.625 · 6.3 ÷ 4 = 1.575.
L4 correct to, one place further: 25 ÷ 6 → 4.2 (1 d.p.) · 47 ÷ 9 → 5.22 (2 d.p.)
· 38 ÷ 7 → 5.429 (3 d.p.; the chopped answer is 5.428).
L5 keep the zero: 35.8 ÷ 6 → 6.0 · 17.52 ÷ 7 → 2.50.
L6 stories: 12 pencils for 87 thousand dong = 7.25 · 50 km in 11 days → 4.55 ·
20.9 kg of rice in 7 bags → 3.0. Numbers are not the deck's, the workbook's or
the quiz's.
