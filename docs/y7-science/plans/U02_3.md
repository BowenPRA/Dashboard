# U02_3 · 2.3 Explaining Changes of State (self-study)

Follows [unit2-close-engines.md](../unit2-close-engines.md) §1–2 (the 2.3 / 2.4 /
2.8 shape) and [ENGAGEMENT-PLAN.md](../ENGAGEMENT-PLAN.md). Reduced from the
classroom lesson `y7-science/U02_3` (Learner's Book pp. 41–43).

**Objective:** use particles to explain why a solid expands and why each change
of state happens — then why solids and liquids cannot be compressed, and why
liquids and gases can flow.
**Classroom twin:** `meta.classroom` → `y7-science/U02_3`; the classroom lesson's
`meta.dashboard` already points back here.

## Explain It (`EXPLAIN_IT`, p73) — `src/utils/stateChain.js`
- **37 scenarios** (expand 7 · melt 6 · freeze 6 · evaporate 6 · boil 5 ·
  condense 7), Vietnamese where natural: mái tôn, trà đá, phở, lẩu, cà phê sữa
  đá, salt fields in Ninh Thuận, a freezing night in Sa Pa. Every expansion is a
  solid, because the chain says "the solid expands".
- **17 links**, worded exactly as the deck's chain cards; `CHAINS`: 3 links for
  expand, 4 for the rest (heat → motion → forces → result).
- **39 traps**, each with its role, the changes it is a real mistake for, and a
  one-sentence why (bilingual): wrong direction, cold energy, temperature
  transferred, particles make heat, energy destroyed, slow/fast/same speed,
  particles bigger/smaller, stop moving, new particles, forces stronger / push
  apart / disappear, evaporation-as-boiling and the reverse, condensing to a
  fixed pattern, freezing as melting, particles melt, new liquid/solid/steam
  particles, disappear, turn into air, bubbles of air, leaks through the glass.
  Every (change, role) has ≥3 traps; no trap reads like a link of a chain it fits.
- `stateBoxSvg` draws solid / solidHot / liquid / gas, particles always radius
  10; the `picture` trap redraws the right state with particles ×1.35 (heating)
  or ×0.68 (cooling).
- Modes: **build** (bank = the chain + 2 traps; the verdict names each trap by
  its step), **fix** (one link swapped for a trap; tap it, then pick from three),
  **name** (heat to/away + one of six changes), **picture** (the after box from
  the right one, the size trap and a wrong state). Sessions deal the six changes
  in shuffled sixes, so ten rounds meet all six, never the same change twice
  running and never a scenario twice.
- `checkExplainConfig` draws 60 random rounds per mode plus every scenario in
  every mode and proves each round's own answer marks right and every wrong
  answer (each trap in each slot, swapped neighbours, wrong pick, wrong
  replacement, each other change, each wrong box) marks wrong with a reason.
- Deck activity `chain` (`ChainActivity.jsx`): `{ ask: 'build' | 'fix',
  scenario, trap? | traps? }`, seeded by the activity id; dense in the side
  column. `BuildBoard` / `FixBoard` are shared with the task screen.

## What was cut or changed, and why
- **Hero:** the paper starter became slide 2's sort; dates gone.
- **"Rulers out — copy this diagram"** → the diagram stays (slide 9) and is
  tapped (slide 10) and labelled (Label It); nothing is copied.
- **Write notes** → orange cards: Key word (definitions) and "The explanation"
  (the chain, exactly as Explain It's links).
- **The classroom diagrams drew particles at different sizes** (the expanding
  solid's particles larger, the liquid's smaller): every particle is now the same
  size in every panel — that is the lesson. BOILING and CONDENSING are drawn at
  640 wide (80 px margins for Label It boxes); BOILING's liquid is a full,
  touching liquid; CONDENSING carries condensing (the room never used it).
- **Added:** EXPAND_SIZE (bigger gaps, not bigger particles), STATE_BOXES (Book
  Q2–3), the bridge estimate, the evaporate/boil/both sort, "three things
  particles never do", the ice-to-steam order.
- **ParticleExplainer** ported: explanation lines reworded to the links, gas
  particles drawn full size (the room drew them at 82%), "Mr Bowen heats" → "An
  iron bar is heated", and the in-widget title hidden on a slide (the slide
  header carries it).
- **"Contract"** is not used: the deck never teaches the word.
