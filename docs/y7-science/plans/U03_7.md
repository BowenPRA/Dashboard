The self-study twin of the classroom deck `y7-science/U03_7` (21 slides, one period,
Learner's Book pp. 117–119). **English and French only** (`meta.vietnamese: false`).
This is the last section of Unit 3 *Forces and energy*. It comes after 3.5 Energy (stores,
transfers, the joule) and 3.6 Changes in energy (energy-change diagrams with boxes and
arrows, heat moving from hotter to colder, the bouncing ball and the candle). Those two
units are being built in parallel, so this plan assumes their content and touches none
of their files.

**Objective:** sort the energy from a change into useful energy (the kind we want) and
wasted energy (the kinds we do not want); count it in joules and percentages, where
useful + wasted = what went in; use the book's percentages to say which of two equally
bright lamps wastes more; explain that wasted energy is dissipated (it spreads out where
it is of no use and can never be recovered) and that some energy is dissipated every time
energy is changed or transferred.

**The thread:** the deck's three commands, *sort it, count it, chase it*. They give the
deck its three parts, and every item in the engine has the same three stages.

**Classroom twin:** `meta.classroom` → `[{ course: 'y7-science', slug: 'U03_7', title:
'Science 3.7 · Where Does Energy Go?' }]`. The lead adds the back-link.

## Where Did It Go? — `ENERGY_SPLIT` (p91), unit key `energySplit`
**Why an engine:** the section's whole skill is one diagram, met with new devices again and
again. The book's Questions 1–3 ask for it; the room did it with thumbs, a game and
whiteboards. No existing task builds an energy-change diagram, splits joules, or marks
where the wasted energy goes. Files: `utils/energySplit.js` (model, stages, slips),
`utils/energySplitData.js` (energies, devices, chase questions), `utils/energySplitCheck.js`
(the proof), `tasks/EnergySplit.jsx`, `components/science/energysplit/` (the diagram board,
the stage panel), and the deck activity `energysplit` in Notes' `SIDE_ACTIVITIES`.

**Tables (data, with French twins):**
- `ENERGIES`: 3.5's eight (chemical, electrical, kinetic, thermal, light, sound,
  gravitational potential, elastic potential). The chips stay in English, with the French
  word small underneath, as the deck's diagrams do.
- `DEVICES`, each with its picture, `from`, `useful`, `wasted`, `want` ("We want hot
  toast"), `notMade` (distractors, each with its own *why*), and a `pct` where there is a
  defensible number. Book numbers: motorbike 25%, filament lamp 15%, energy-saving lamp
  50%. 3.6's bouncing ball: 55%. Rounded real-world values: kettle 90%, phone charging
  80%, cycling 25%, walking up stairs 20%, cooking on a wood fire about 10%. No numbers for
  the toaster, radio, TV, candle or fan: their real values are either tiny or unknown, and
  I won't invent them.
  Where one energy is both useful and wasted (the fire's heat), the chips say where it
  goes: "thermal (the water)" / "thermal (the pot, the air)".
- `CHASE`: two questions, `where` (an hour later, where is the wasted heat?) and
  `recover` (can it be collected and used again?). Each option is tied to a named slip.

**Item:** `{ id, level, device, total?, ask?: 'useful' | 'wasted' | 'in', given?, chase?:
'where' | 'recover', walk? }` · `{ id, level, kind: 'pair', a, b, useful }` (the same light).

**Stages, all derived from the tables:**
- *Sort it*, walked as three questions like the deck's ThreeProcesses rows: **From**
  (where does the energy come from?) → **Useful** (what do we want?) → **Wasted** (what
  else comes out?), each by tapping chips onto 3.6's diagram (one box, a green arrow, a
  red arrow).
- *Count it* (only when the item has numbers): **Useful J** → **Wasted J**, or **In J**
  for an inverse item. A `pair` item counts both lamps' joules in, then **Decide**: which
  wastes more, and by how much.
- *Chase it* (only when the item has `chase`): one question. It is never skipped.

**The fading rule (as in 5.1):** levels 1–2 walk every stage. From level 3 an item opens
**quick**: the whole diagram at once (drag every chip into its box, type the one number
asked), then one Check.
- All right: done, clean.
- A wrong part: its slip is named and the stage it belongs to opens. A wrong source opens
  From, a wrong chip opens Useful or Wasted, a wrong number opens its count stage. The
  stages before it are marked as given.

**Named slips:**
- *Sort:* `want` (a useful energy marked wasted or the other way round; the message is the
  device's own *want* line), `notMade` (a distractor, with its own why; for example "a
  flat road: you do not get any higher"), `missed` (one left out), `fromOutput` (an output
  chosen as the source), `fromWrong` (electrical for a motorbike).
- *Count:* `pctAsJ` (the % typed as joules when the total is not 100), `swap` (useful and
  wasted swapped), `noSub` (the total typed as the wasted), `added` (total + useful),
  `halved` (inverse: ÷ 2 instead of × 2), `outAsIn` (the output typed as the input).
- *Chase:* `gone` (used up), `stays` (still in the device), `back` (back in its store),
  `bag` (it can be caught and kept), `destroyed`.

Each wrong answer is named and the student tries again. A second wrong (or Show me) fills
the stage in, and the item pays ½. Blob `{ id: 1 | 0.5 }`, out of 10.

**Levels (14 items):**

| Level | Name | Items |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sort it (walked, no numbers) | candle in a power cut (chase `where`) · radio |
| 2 | Count it (walked, 100 J) | the motorbike, 25% (chase `recover`: Mr Bowen's bag) · lamp B, 50% |
| 3 | What do we want? (quick, sort only: the traps) | toaster (heat useful, glow wasted) · phone charging (chemical is the *useful* output) · cycling on a flat road (gravitational potential as a distractor) |
| 4 | Bigger numbers (quick + one number) | lamp A at 200 J (wasted 170) · the kettle at 1000 J (wasted 100) |
| 5 | The same light | lamp B gives 15 J of light: how much goes in? (walked, it is new) · the pair: lamps A and B for the same 15 J, so 100 J against 30 J, and A wastes 70 J more |
| 6 | Chase it | the wood fire (two kinds of thermal; chase `where`) · 3.6's ball (100 J in, 55 J back up: how much dissipated, and where?) · the walk up the stairs at 1000 J, 20% |

**Proof:** `checkEnergySplitItems` (at the `@@SCI_U03_7_CHECK@@` marker) and
`checkEnergySplitActivity` (the two deck activities). Every stage accepts its own answer,
and every slip fires on its canonical wrong answer. The check refuses:
- a slip equal to the answer, or two slips sharing a value in one stage;
- joules that are not whole numbers, or a % outside 1–99;
- a distractor that the device really makes;
- a device or chase option without its French.

The same check runs in Node over every device × total from {100, 200, 400, 1000} × ask.
