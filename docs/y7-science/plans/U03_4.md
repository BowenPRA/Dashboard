point at `Y7_SCI/U03_4`. **English and French only** (`meta.vietnamese: false`).

**Objective:** a tide is the change in the depth of the sea during the day, and the tidal
range is high tide minus low tide; the Moon's tidal force stretches the oceans into two
bulges, so a coast has two high tides and two low tides a day as the Earth spins (high to
low about 6 hours, high to high about 12); high tide comes about 50 minutes later each day;
the Sun pulls harder but stretches the Earth less, and the biggest tides come at new moon
and full moon, when the Sun, the Moon and the Earth are in a line; effects of tides on the
sea and on the land; and, from the lab, reading a water level accurately, describing the
tide as coming in or going out, and testing the two predictions against readings.

**The thread:** Mr Bowen's harbour (the deck) and our river (the lab). The Moon makes the
predictions; the river's readings test them.

## The call: the lab becomes the flagship task

The lab is not a Cambridge section, so it gets no unit of its own, and earlier labs
(`U03_lab`) got no twin. This one is different. Its real work is **data**: read a scale,
record it, graph it, describe it, and test a prediction against it. That is exactly what
3.4 sets up and never does. The book's Activity ends on two predictions from the Moon
model (about 50 minutes later every day; the biggest tides at new moon and full moon), and
the lab exists to test them. A student alone cannot stand at our river, but they can do
every one of those decisions on screen, with a river the 3.4 model generates fresh each
time. So the flagship is **Tide Lab**: the lab, at home, marked.

- **Kept from the lab:** reading the pole (marks, between marks, waves), the results table
  with the unit in the heading, the line graph, coming in / going out, the two predictions
  and testing them, Mr Bowen's four bad readings, the Bến Tre king tide.
- **Room-only, cut:** standing at the river, the calendar of 44 readings, drawing the table
  in the notebook, the safety rules at the bank, the class's own results table and graph
  (they live in the classroom computer's browser), the whiteboard sketch, both hand votes
  (they are 3.4's own questions), "for 12 November".
- **Not used:** the class's real readings. They are not reachable from the Dashboard, and
  half of them are not taken yet. The engine's river is the 3.4 model (two tides a day,
  50 minutes later each day, range set by the Moon), read at the lab's four times.

## Tide Lab (`TIDE_LAB`, p88): `utils/tideLab.js`, `tasks/TideLab.jsx`
**The river.** Pure functions in `tideLab.js` make a river from a seed: readings at 8:30,
10:30, 12:30 and 2:30, rounded to 5 cm (what a pole marked every 10 cm can show); the level
follows two tides a day (12 h 25 min), high tide 50 minutes later each school day, and the
day's range set by the Moon (big at new and full moon, small at the half moons), as in the
deck's `orbit.js`. Each attempt draws a new river (the seed is kept in the blob, so a resumed
item is the same river). The pole is the lab's: marked every 10 cm, numbered every 20 cm.

**Screens.** The results table always has the unit in the heading ("Water level (cm)"), so
the student types numbers only. The graph is the lab's: a band per day, four dots joined
left to right, no line overnight.

**Item kinds and stages (all derived):**
- `pole` — **Read** the pole into the table: on a numbered mark, on an unnumbered mark,
  halfway between two marks, or with waves (read the middle).
- `day` — one day's four readings. **Plot** (tap a cross at each time; it snaps to 5 cm) →
  **In or out?** (coming in / going out between two times) → **Highest** (tap the time) →
  **Change** (highest − lowest, typed).
- `later` — Prediction 1. **Tomorrow** (high tide at 11:00 today; tomorrow?) → **Test it**
  (a week's graph: tap the highest reading on three days) → **Verdict** (supports / seems
  to contradict / proves it) → **Why** (a reason bank: the Moon moves on along its orbit,
  so the Earth turns a little more to face it ✓, plus traps).
- `biggest` — Prediction 2. Two weeks with the Moon on the calendar. **Change** (a day in
  each week) → **Which week?** → **The Moon** (what was it like that week?) → **Verdict** →
  **Why** (in a line, the Sun's tide adds to the Moon's ✓, plus traps).

**Named slips:** `below` / `above` (the nearest numbered mark), `count` (each small mark read
as 1 cm: 121 for 130), `crest` / `trough` (the top or bottom of the wave) · `swap` (a cross
at another time's reading), `line` (one grid line out) · `reversed` (in for out) ·
`lowest`, `last` (the last reading taken as the highest) · `ends` (last − first instead of
highest − lowest), `value` (the highest reading given as the change) · `same`, `earlier`,
`twelve` (12 hours later is tonight's high tide, not tomorrow's) · `pick` (not the day's
highest) · `contradicts`, `proves` (data supports a prediction; it does not prove it, as in
3.2) · `smaller` (the week with the smaller change), `halfmoon` · traps for Why: `slowspin`
(the Earth spins more slowly each day), `sunback` (the Sun pulls the water back), `closer`
(the Moon is closer at full moon), `stronger` (a new moon has stronger gravity), `sunharder`
(the Sun pulls harder at new moon), `seeit` (we can see the full moon, so it pulls more).

**Fading.** Levels 1–2 walk every stage; later items are 2–3 actions. A wrong answer is
named and tried once more, and opens the stage under its slip: a `ends` or `value` change
opens **Find them** (tap the highest and the lowest reading, then subtract); a `pick` opens
the day's four readings as a table. A second wrong (or Show me) fills the stage in, and the
item pays half. Blob `{ id: 1 | 0.5 }` plus each item's seed.

**12 items, 6 levels:**
| Level | Items | Actions |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Read the pole | p1 on a numbered mark · p2 on an unnumbered mark | 1 each |
| 2 Between the marks | p3 halfway · d1 a day: Plot → In or out → Highest | 1 · 6 |
| 3 A day's tide | d2 In or out → Highest → Change · d3 Highest → Change | 3 · 2 |
| 4 Waves, and the end of the day | p4 waves · d4 Change → "coming in or going out at 2:30?" | 1 · 2 |
| 5 Prediction 1 | l1 Tomorrow → Test it → Verdict → Why · l2 Test it → Why | 4 · 2 |
| 6 Prediction 2 | b1 Change ×2 → Which week → The Moon → Verdict → Why · b2 Which week → Why | 6 · 2 |

**Proof:** `checkTideConfig` (validator marker) runs every item over 200 seeds: the
derived answer is accepted, each named slip fires on its canonical wrong answer, no two
slips share a wrong answer (the river is re-drawn if they would), every stage has an answer
the picture can show (no hidden readings), and every table entry has its French twin.
