3.1 Gravity (Weigh It) and 3.2 Formation (Storyboard), and repeats neither: 3.1 owns weight,
mass and N/kg, and 3.2 owns how the planets formed and why they all go round the same way.
3.3 owns three questions, the book's three objectives.

**Objective:** the Sun's gravity keeps the planets in orbit and always pulls **towards the
Sun**; without it a planet would go off in a **straight line**, the way it was moving (not
outwards); a bar graph of the orbit speeds shows that **speed decreases as the distance from
the Sun increases**, because the Sun's pull is weaker far away; **air resistance** slows
moving things on Earth, but space is a **vacuum**, so nothing slows the planets down and only
gravity acts on them.

**The thread:** "Let go of the string." The ball on Mr Bowen's string is the model for the
whole deck: the string is gravity (what keeps it going round), letting go shows what gravity
does (without it, straight on), and the ball that drops to the floor in the room, but not
in space, is the vacuum.

## Orbit Lab (`ORBIT_LAB`, p87): `utils/orbitLab.js`, `tasks/OrbitLab.jsx`
**Why an engine:** each of the book's three questions is a skill a student must use on
things they have not met, and each has a named wrong idea to catch: the outward fling, "a
bigger planet is slower", "no gravity in space", and "it needs a push to keep moving". No
existing task draws a direction on an orbit, builds a bar graph, or sorts forces by where
the air is. One item kind per book objective, the way Storyboard has three kinds.
**Decision:** `coast` is the kind closest to MCQ. If you would rather keep the engine to
`pull` + `graph` (9 items), objective 3 stays with the deck (slides 11–14), Short Answers
sq3 and two quiz questions.

**Kind `pull`: the book's p. 92 diagram, anywhere on any orbit.**
`{ id, level, kind: 'pull', scene, at, dir: 'acw' | 'cw', ask? }`. Scenes: a planet round
the Sun, the Moon round the Earth, Mr Bowen's ball on the string, a comet on a long orbit
(the Sun at one end), and Uranus with Neptune further out. The object sits at one of eight
places on its orbit. Eight arrows around it: tap one.
- *Pull* ("which way does the Sun's gravity pull it?"): towards the centre. Slips: `away`,
  `along` (the way it is moving: "gravity pushes it round"), `against`, `middle` (comet:
  the middle of the orbit, not the Sun), `sun` (Neptune's pull drawn towards the Sun),
  `off` (a diagonal).
- *Let go* ("gravity switches off now"): straight on, along the orbit's edge, forwards.
  Slips: `away` (straight out: the vote's A), `inward` (it falls in), `backwards` (it went
  the wrong way round), `off`.
- *Why* (walked, or after a Let go slip): "nothing pulls it towards the Sun any more, so it
  keeps going the way it was moving" ✓ · traps `fling` (a force throws it outwards),
  `push` (gravity was pushing it along; now it stops), `fall` (it falls into the Sun).
- Every key direction is derived from the geometry (position, direction of travel, where
  the pulling body is), and the validator refuses an item whose key directions do not land
  exactly on four different arrows. The comet sits where the Sun is at 45° and the middle
  of the orbit is straight below, so `middle` is a real choice. For Neptune (1822, the two
  planets in line), the right arrow is the `away` arrow of every earlier item: gravity
  pulls towards whatever has the mass.

**Kind `graph`: the book's Activity, on new data.**
`{ id, level, kind: 'graph', set, given?, ask }`. Real orbit speeds (rounded, km/s):
the planets (48 … 5, the book's), Jupiter's four big moons (Io 17, Europa 14, Ganymede 11,
Callisto 8), Saturn's moons (Mimas 14 … Titan 6, Iapetus 3), and things that orbit the
Earth (the ISS 8, a GPS satellite 4, a TV satellite 3, the Moon 1). Each set has its own
scale (1 square = 5, 2 or 1 km/s).
- *Draw*: drag each bar's top (or ▲▼) on squared paper with small lines of 1 km/s, like
  millimetres on graph paper, and no read-out on the bar. A bar must reach its value
  exactly, which is the book's "1 cm = 5 km/s, so 48 km/s is 9.6 cm". A wrong bar gets
  its right height drawn as a ghost, with the working ("48 = 45 + 3: 9 big squares and 3
  small lines"). Slips: `bar`, `swap` (two bars swapped), `scale` (every bar out by the
  same factor).
- *Pattern*: "As the distance from ___ increases, the speed ___" (increases / **decreases**
  / stays the same). Slips `up`, `same`.
- *Why*: the pull is weaker far away ✓ · traps per set, each refuted from that set's own
  data: `size` (Jupiter, the biggest planet, is faster than Saturn, Uranus and Neptune;
  Ganymede is bigger than Callisto, and faster). It is never used for the Earth's set,
  where the Moon is both the biggest and the slowest. `path` (a longer path makes a longer
  year, not a lower speed), `air`, `cold`, `engines` (satellites have no engines pushing
  them round).
- *Predict*: a real object between two in the set (Hyperion, between Titan and Iapetus;
  a weather satellite, between the ISS and GPS) → faster than all / **between the two** /
  slower than all. Slip `pattern`.
- The validator proves each set is sorted by distance with strictly falling speeds, every
  value fits its axis and lands on a small line, no `size` trap sits on a set that
  supports it, and the predicted object really lies in its gap.

**Kind `coast`: what slows it down?**
`{ id, level, kind: 'coast', scene }`. Scenes: a space probe drifting far from any star or
planet, its engine off; the Moon going round the Earth; a bicycle free-wheeling on a flat
road; a plane rolling along the runway after landing (the spoilers photo). Each picture
has a movement arrow.
- *Slow it?* ("which forces act **against** its movement?"): tap the chips Air resistance ·
  Friction · Gravity · Nothing. The answer is derived from where the scene is: in space,
  Nothing (the Moon's gravity pulls it **sideways**, towards the Earth, which bends its path
  but does not slow it); on the road, air resistance and friction. Slips: `air` (air in
  space), `friction` (nothing touches it), `gravity` (gravity against a sideways movement),
  `noair`, `nofriction`, `nothing`.
- *Speed*: the result and its reason, chosen together. Space: "it keeps the same speed:
  nothing pushes against it" ✓ · `push` (it slows down and stops: nothing pushes it
  forwards) · `nogravity` (it keeps its speed **because there is no gravity in space**:
  right result, wrong reason) · `runout` (the push it was given runs out). Road: "it slows
  down: air resistance and friction push against its movement" ✓ · `noforce` · `gravity`
  · `runout`.
- No scene in which gravity really slows the object (Voyager, a thrown ball) is used: the
  marking would have to be "almost". Those go in the deck, with their explanation.

**The fading rule.** Levels 1–2 walk every stage, with a one-line hint per stage. From level
3 an item opens **quick**: *pull* is both arrows on one picture (tap the pull, tap the path,

and opens the stage it belongs to (a Let go slip also opens *Why*). A second wrong answer
or Show me fills the stage in, and the item pays ½. Blob `{ id: 1 | 0.5 }`, out of 12.
