# U03_5 · 3.5 Energy (self-study)

> Built 3 Nov 2026 as planned and approved ("Approved, build it"). The changes made
> while building are under **As built** at the end.

The self-study twin of the classroom deck `y7-science/U03_5` (22 slides, one period,
Learner's Book pp. 105–110), after its clarity pass. **English and French only**
(`meta.vietnamese: false`). `meta.classroom` →
`[{ course: 'y7-science', slug: 'U03_5', title: 'Science 3.5 · Energy' }]`; the lesson's
`meta.dashboard` back-link is added by the lead at integration.

**Objective:** say what energy is and measure it in joules; name the five energy stores
(kinetic, chemical, thermal, elastic potential, gravitational potential) and the three
transfers (electrical, sound, light; thermal is both) **in a situation I have not seen
before, with the clue for each**; and explain why chemical and gravitational potential
stores last a long time while thermal and kinetic stores do not.

**The thread:** the deck's three questions — **how much? what kind? how long?** — with
Mr Bowen's coffee running through all three (the kettle, the cold coffee, the banana).
The real thinking of the section is the middle one: look at something, find the clue
(moving, lifted up high, hot, stretched or squashed, food/fuel/battery, a current,
vibrating, luminous), name the energy, and say whether it is a store or a transfer. That is
the flagship engine.

**Scope against 3.6 and 3.7** (built in parallel, next in the sequence): this unit
*names* the energy at each place. It never draws an energy-change diagram or says "from
the chemical store to the kinetic store" (3.6), and never says wasted, useful or
efficiency (3.7). The cold coffee says only what the book's 3.5 says: its heat goes into
the room in about an hour, so a thermal store does not last.

## Name That Energy (`ENERGY_SPOT`, p89)
**Why an engine:** the book's own work (Q1, Q2, the bus, the poster Activity) is "look at it
and name every store and transfer". No existing task asks for the clue, the energy *and*
the family, and none names the misconception behind a wrong answer. Files:
`utils/energySpotData.js` (tables), `utils/energySpot.js` (derive + mark),
`utils/energySpotCheck.js` (the proof), `tasks/EnergySpot.jsx` +
`components/science/energySpot/` (the stages). Unit key `energySpot` in data.js; its
pictures are the unit's `art.js` drawings, passed in data.js (as 3.2 passes its frames).

**Tables.**
- `ENERGIES` — the eight, each `{ en, fr, family: store | transfer, pose }` (thermal is
  both: a thing's clue says which); teal for stores, purple for transfers, as on the deck.
- `CLUES` — the book's table lines, each → one energy: moving · food, fuel or a battery · hot
  · stretched or squashed · lifted up high · a current in a wire · vibrating · gives out its
  own light (luminous) · heat moving from hot to colder.
- `THINGS` — every picture or pin: `{ en, fr, clue }`. **The energy, its family and whether it
  lasts are derived from the clue**, never written twice.
- `TRAPS` — named misconceptions, each with a one-sentence why (EN/FR), keyed by the right
  energy and the wrong one chosen: `highMoving` (a still thing up high called kinetic),
  `movingHigh`, `battery` (a battery "stores electricity"), `wire` (the wire stores
  energy), `stretchMove` (a stretched thing called kinetic), `vibrateMove` (a drum called
  kinetic), `hotLight` / `lightHot`, `foodHot` (food is chemical, hot or cold),
  `transferStore` (sound, light or electrical called a store), `storeTransfer`,
  `soundStore` (the falling rock's sound), `missed` (a store left out, named by its clue),
  `heatLasts`, `moveLasts`, `chemGoes`, `gpeGoes`, `hotMore` ("hot means more energy"). A
  wrong answer with no trap gets `other`, which names the clue the chosen energy would need.

**Item kinds.**
- `one` — one picture, "Which energy?". Stages: **Look** (which clue do you see? the right
  line + 3) → **Name** (the eight cards) → **Store or transfer?**
- `scene` — a drawing with 3–8 numbered pins, each leader on a clue (the bus keeps the deck's
  geometry). Tap a pin, tap an energy (a card can be used twice, or not at all); then **tap
  the stores** (one action). A wrong pin names its slip and opens Look for that pin only.
- `stores` — "Which stores does it have? Tap all of them." The transfers are among the cards
  on purpose.
- `lasts` — **Store** (which of the five?) → **A year from now?** (still there / gone) →
  **Why** (a reason from a bank of right reasons and traps). Asked only of the four stores the
  book classifies — never elastic potential.

**The fading rule.** Levels 1–2 walk every stage. From level 3 an item opens **quick**: the
Name and the family together (2 taps), or for `lasts` the store and the verdict together. A
wrong name names its slip and opens Look → Name; a wrong family opens only Store or
transfer. Each answer is named and tried again; a second wrong, or Show me, fills the stage
in and the item pays ½. Blob `{ id: 1 | 0.5 }`.

**Levels (14 items).**
1. `one`, walked: rice · a water tower (the game's pictures).
2. `one`, walked: a drum · a squashed spring.
3. `one`, quick, new to the student: a battery (`battery`) · a car driving · a bent bow.
4. `scene`: the circuit (battery, wire, bulb: the book's Q1, 3 pins) · Mr Bowen's desk (the
   kettle's hot water, the lamp, the phone's charging cable, his banana: 4 pins).
5. `stores` a falling rock (kinetic + GPE; `soundStore`) · `lasts` a hot bath (`heatLasts`) ·
   `lasts` water behind a dam.
6. `scene` the bus going uphill (8 pins, the book's picture) · `stores` a hot pie on a high
   shelf (thermal, chemical, GPE).

Pictures: the ported `art.js` (rice, waterTower, drum, spring, battery, car, bow, bulb,
charging, kettle, banana, cliffRock, mug, dam, bus); new in the same style: a hot bath, a pie
on a shelf, the desk scene (composed from art.js pieces).
