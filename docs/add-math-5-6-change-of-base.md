# Add Maths 5.6 — Change of base of logarithms

Source section: Cambridge IGCSE and O Level Additional Mathematics coursebook (3rd
edition), section 5.6, pages 112–114. Read in the Cambridge GO reader on 2026-09-30.

Three parts: the beats of the notes in our own words, a map of the exercise's question
types, and a fresh problem set that covers every type, with answers. None of the
problems are the book's. Every answer was checked by script.

## Part 1 — The beats of the notes

1. **Why (p. 112).** Sometimes a log is in a base you cannot work with, so you rewrite
   it in another.
2. **The rule.** log_b a = log_c a ÷ log_c b, for positive a, b, c with b and c not 1.
3. **Proof.** Call the log x, write it as a power, take logs to base c of both sides,
   bring the power down, divide.
4. **The reciprocal case.** Choosing the new base to be a gives log_b a = 1 ÷ log_a b.
5. **Worked example 12 (p. 113).** Change a log to base 10 and evaluate it to 3 s.f. A
   note says some calculators have a key that does this directly.
6. **Worked example 13.** An equation with two related bases (one is the square of the
   other). Change one log to the smaller base, clear the fraction, use the power law,
   equate, solve the quadratic, check both roots. One is rejected.

## Part 2 — What the exercise asks

Exercise 5.6 is on pages 113–114: twelve questions, the last marked as challenge.

| Type | Book question | What it asks | How it climbs |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Evaluate | 1 (four parts) | Use the rule with base 10, answer to 3 s.f. | Ends with a small decimal giving a negative answer |

| E. A product of logs | 5 | Evaluate a product where the bases chain | One question |
| F. Equations with several bases | 6 (two parts) | Evaluate the numerical logs first, then solve | Two parts |
| G. Guided substitution | 7 | Express one log in terms of another base, then substitute and solve | Two parts |
| H. Two related bases | 8 (four parts) | Solve, changing to the smaller base | Sums and differences with coefficients |
| I. Guided reciprocal | 9 | Express log_x of a number in the other base, then substitute and solve | Two parts |

| K. Guided simultaneous | 11 | Two "express" parts, then a pair of equations | Three parts |

## Part 3 — Fresh problems in the same style

### A. Evaluate

Use the change of base rule to evaluate, correct to 3 significant figures.

- A1. log₂ 13
- A2. log₃ 50
- A3. log₅ 2
- A4. log₇ 300
- A5. log₄ 0.3
- A6. log₆ 0.0045

### B. In terms of u

Given that u = log₃ x, find in simplest form in terms of u:

- B1. logₓ 3
- B2. logₓ 9
- B3. logₓ 27
- B4. logₓ √3
- B5. logₓ (1/3)
- B6. log₉ x

### C. Express in terms of x

Given that log₄ y = x, express in terms of x:

- C1. log_y 4
- C2. log₄ (16y)
- C3. log₂ y
- C4. log₂ (8y)
- C5. log₁₆ y
- C6. log_y 2

### D. One log from two

- D1. Given that log_p x = 12 and log_p y = 4, find log_y x.
- D2. Given that log_p X = 9 and log_p Y = 6, find log_X Y.
- D3. Given that log_a b = 3 and log_b c = 4, find log_a c.

### E. A product of logs

Evaluate:

- E1. log_p 3 × log₉ p
- E2. log_p 8 × log₂ p
- E3. log₂ 3 × log₃ 4
- E4. log₅ 7 × log₇ 25

### F. Equations with several bases

Solve:

- F1. log₄ 2 + log₄ (x + 1) = log₃ 9
- F2. 3 log₈ 2 + log₅ (2x − 1) = log₂ 8
- F3. log₆ (x + 3) + log₉ 3 = log₄ 8
- F4. log₂ x = log₄ (x + 12)
- F5. log₃ (x − 2) = log₉ (x + 4)

### G. Guided substitution

- G1. 1. Express log₉ x in terms of log₃ x.
  2. Using your answer and the substitution u = log₃ x, solve log₉ x + log₃ x = 6.

### H. Two related bases

Solve:

- H1. log₂ x + 3 log₄ x = 10
- H2. log₃ x + 4 log₉ x = 6
- H3. 3 log₂ x − log₈ x = 8
- H4. 2 log₅ x = log₂₅ x + 3
- H5. log₂ x + log₄ x + log₈ x = 11

### I. Guided reciprocal

- I1. 1. Express logₓ 2 in terms of a logarithm to base 2.
  2. Using your answer and the substitution u = log₂ x, solve log₂ x = 5 − 4 logₓ 2.

### J. Reciprocal equations

Solve:

- J1. log₂ x = 4 logₓ 2
- J2. log₇ x + logₓ 7 = 2.5
- J3. log₃ x − 6 logₓ 3 + 1 = 0
- J4. log₂ x + 8 logₓ 2 − 6 = 0
- J5. log₅ x − 3 logₓ 5 = 2
- J6. log₄ y = 3 − 2 log_y 4

### K. Guided simultaneous

- K1. 1. Express log₉ x in terms of log₃ x.
  2. Express log₂₇ y in terms of log₃ y.
  3. Hence solve the simultaneous equations
     4 log₉ x + 3 log₂₇ y = 7 and log₃ x − 2 log₉ y = 2.

### L. Challenge-style

- L1. Solve the simultaneous equations 2 log₂ y = log₃ 9 + log₂ x and 3ʸ = 9ˣ.
- L2. Show that log_a b × log_b c × log_c a = 1.

## Answers

**A.** A1 3.70. A2 3.56. A3 0.431. A4 2.93. A5 −0.868. A6 −3.02.

**B.** B1 1/u. B2 2/u. B3 3/u. B4 1/(2u). B5 −1/u. B6 u/2.

**C.** C1 1/x. C2 2 + x. C3 2x. C4 3 + 2x. C5 x/2. C6 1/(2x).

**D.** D1 12 ÷ 4 = 3. D2 6 ÷ 9 = 2/3. D3 3 × 4 = 12.

**E.** E1 ½. E2 3. E3 log₂ 4 = 2. E4 log₅ 25 = 2.

**F.** F1 ½ + log₄ (x + 1) = 2, so x + 1 = 8 and x = 7. F2 1 + log₅ (2x − 1) = 3, so
x = 13. F3 log₆ (x + 3) + ½ = 3/2, so x = 3. F4 2 log₂ x = log₂ (x + 12), so
x² − x − 12 = 0 and x = 4 (−3 rejected). F5 (x − 2)² = x + 4, so x = 5 (x = 0 rejected:
it gives a log of −2).

**G.** G1 log₉ x = ½ log₃ x. Then 1.5u = 6, u = 4, x = 81.

**H.** H1 2.5u = 10, u = 4, x = 16. H2 3u = 6, u = 2, x = 9. H3 (8/3)u = 8, u = 3,
x = 8. H4 1.5u = 3, u = 2, x = 25. H5 (11/6)u = 11, u = 6, x = 64.

**I.** I1 logₓ 2 = 1 ÷ log₂ x. Then u² − 5u + 4 = 0, u = 1 or 4, x = 2 or 16.

**J.** J1 u² = 4, u = ±2, x = 4 or ¼. J2 2u² − 5u + 2 = 0, u = 2 or ½, x = 49 or √7.
J3 u² + u − 6 = 0, u = 2 or −3, x = 9 or 1/27. J4 u² − 6u + 8 = 0, u = 2 or 4, x = 4
or 16. J5 u² − 2u − 3 = 0, u = 3 or −1, x = 125 or 1/5. J6 u² − 3u + 2 = 0, u = 1 or 2,
y = 4 or 16.

**K.** K1 log₉ x = ½ log₃ x and log₂₇ y = ⅓ log₃ y. With u = log₃ x and v = log₃ y:
2u + v = 7 and u − v = 2, so u = 3, v = 1. x = 27, y = 3.

**L.** L1 log₃ 9 = 2, so log₂ y² = log₂ 4x and y² = 4x. The second equation gives
y = 2x. Then 4x² = 4x, so x = 1 (x = 0 rejected) and y = 2.
L2 Change every log to base 10: (lg b ÷ lg a)(lg c ÷ lg b)(lg a ÷ lg c) = 1.

## Notes for building

- This section is planned for `AM_5C`, with 5.4.
- More than any other section, the book leans on guided questions here (types G, I and
  K). They teach the substitution before the unguided set that follows each one.
- Every type J equation has two valid roots, one of which is often a fraction or a
  surd. There is nothing to reject, which makes a good contrast with 5.4.
