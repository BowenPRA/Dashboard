# 6.1 Acids, Bases and Alkalis — unit plan (IGCSE_CHEM / M06_1)

Wolsey Hall Module 6, Topic One (6.1). Built from book spreads **11.1 "Acids and
bases"** and **11.2 "A closer look at acids and alkalis"** (pages 128–131), with
the Chapter 11 Checkup (page 144) as the guide to what the exam asks.
English-only (`bilingual: false`), so no `vn*` twins.

Stops before 6.2 (`M06_2`): nothing here on the reactions of acids, salts or
neutralisation.

## Objective

The student can say what an acid is (a non-metal compound that gives H⁺ in
water); name the four lab acids and give their formulae (H₂SO₄, HCl, HNO₃,
CH₃COOH); tell **dilute** from **concentrated** and say what **corrosive**
means; name the acids in lemons, yoghurt and fizzy drinks; define a **base**
(metal oxide or hydroxide) and an **alkali** (a soluble base), and name the
three lab alkalis and limewater; give the colours of litmus, methyl orange and
thymolphthalein in acid and in alkali; name **H⁺** as the ion of acidity and
**OH⁻** as the ion of alkalinity, with the dissociation equations for HCl and
NaOH; use the **pH scale** (below 7 acidic, 7 neutral, above 7 alkaline) and
link pH to H⁺ / OH⁻ concentration; read universal indicator and say when to use a
pH meter. **Extended:** strong acids are completely dissociated, weak acids
partially (the ⇌ sign for ethanoic acid); the same concentration can give a
different pH (0.1 mol/dm³ HCl pH 1.08, ethanoic acid pH 2.88); and strength is
not concentration.

## The mistakes the unit is built around

| Mistake | Where it is caught |
|---|---|
| Calling every base an alkali (an insoluble oxide counted as an alkali) | deck c4, workbook f1 and p3, quiz 1, Questions sq1 |
| Treating any oxide as a base (a non-metal oxide) | deck sort a1 |
| Mixing up the indicator colours, or reading the wrong column | deck c6, c7, sort a2, c13; quiz 4; Source Analysis 2; Questions sq2 |
| "acidic" / "alkaline" used as nouns ("an alkaline") | English slide c5 |
| More H⁺ read as a higher pH | deck c10, workbook p1, quiz 6, Source Analysis 1, Questions sq4 |
| Thinking below-7-but-close (milk, rain) is neutral | deck sort a4 |
| Using an indicator when a precise pH is wanted | quiz 8 |
| Assuming the same concentration gives the same pH | deck predict a6, workbook c1 and c3, Source Analysis 3 |
| Giving a weak acid a one-way arrow, or wrong charges on the ions | deck c12, workbook c2, quiz 5 and 9 |
| Reading "weak" as "dilute", "strong" as "concentrated" | deck sort a7 (its own slide), quiz 10, Questions sq5 |
| Charges not balanced; H₂SO₄ as HSO₄; Ca(OH)₂ as CaOH₂ | Formulae task |

## The deck (21 slides)

1 opener (what do sour foods have in common?) · 2 the four lab acids,
`LAB_ACIDS` (c1) · 3 dilute, concentrated, corrosive, `CORROSIVE` (c2) · 4 acids
you eat and drink — citric, lactic, carbonic (c3) · 5 bases: oxides and
hydroxides of metals (**sort a1**: base or not) · 6 alkalis are the soluble
bases, limewater, `BASES_ALKALIS` (c4) · 7 **English:** acid / acidic, alkali /
alkaline (c5) · 8 the litmus test, `LITMUS` (c6) · 9 three indicators,
`INDICATOR_TABLE` (c7) · 10 from memory (**sort a2**: indicator result → acid or
alkali) · 11 what all acids have in common, HCl(aq) → H⁺ + Cl⁻, `ION_BADGES` (c8)
· 12 what all alkalis have in common, NaOH(aq) → Na⁺ + OH⁻ (c9) · 13 the pH scale,
`PH_SCALE` (c10) · 14 **estimate a3**: the pH of stomach acid (≈ 2) · 15 acidic,
neutral or alkaline (**sort a4**: seven solutions by pH) · 16 universal indicator
and the pH meter (**order a5**: indicator colours, most acidic → most alkaline) ·
**Extended:** 17 **predict a6**: same concentration, same pH? · 18 strong and
weak acids, `STRONG_WEAK` (c11) · 19 the two-way arrow, CH₃COOH ⇌ H⁺ + CH₃COO⁻
(c12) · 20 strong is not the same as concentrated, `CONC_STRENGTH` (**sort a7**:
strength or concentration) · 21 countable recap, "Can you do all six?" (c13).

Activities by type: sort ×4, estimate, order, predict. The predict (17) comes
before the slide that answers it (18). The indicator sort (10) and the colour
order (16) sit on slides without the table or the scale, so they are recall, not
reading. The book's worked examples (HCl and NaOH dissociating, 0.1 mol/dm³ HCl
and ethanoic acid) are taught in the deck; the tasks use fresh substances
(KOH, LiOH, HNO₃, methanoic acid).

## Diagrams (authored, no third-party imagery)

`diagrams.js` holds nine: `LAB_ACIDS` (four bottles with formulae), `CORROSIVE`
(the hazard sign, drawn), `BASES_ALKALIS` (alkalis as a box inside bases),
`LITMUS` (blue paper in acid, red paper in alkali), `INDICATOR_TABLE` (three
indicators, real colours), `ION_BADGES` (H⁺ and OH⁻ badges with the two
dissociation equations), `PH_SCALE` (0–14 in universal-indicator colours, with
the H⁺ / OH⁻ arrows), `STRONG_WEAK` (0.1 mol/dm³ HCl all ions vs ethanoic acid
mostly molecules), `CONC_STRENGTH` (2 × 2: strong / weak × concentrated /
dilute).

Colour: acid / H⁺ red `#c8102e`, alkali / OH⁻ blue-violet `#4338ca`, neutral
green `#2f8f5b`, the other ion of an acid grey `#94a3b8`. Indicator chips and
litmus papers use the colours the indicators really turn.

## Formulae (`FORMULA_WRITE`, 10 items)

HCl · HNO₃ · H₂SO₄ (the acids as H⁺ compounds) · NaOH · KOH · CaO · MgO · CuO ·
Ca(OH)₂ · Mg(OH)₂. Every item checked neutral and in lowest terms with a node
script. The engine heads the positive slot "Metal ion (＋)"; the three acid
hints say that hydrogen is not a metal but H⁺ goes there.
