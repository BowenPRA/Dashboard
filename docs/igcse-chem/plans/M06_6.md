# 6.6 Group 1: The Alkali Metals — unit plan (IGCSE_CHEM / M06_6)

Wolsey Hall Module 6, Topic Six (6.6). Built from book spread **12.2 "Group I:
the alkali metals"** (pages 148–149), with point 4 of **12.4** (why reactivity
increases down Group I) and the Group I row of its ion-charge table. The Chapter
12 Checkup (page 158) decides what is Core and what is Extended. English-only
(`bilingual: false`), so no `vn*` twins.

Recaps 6.5 (the Periodic Table, 12.1) in one slide; ends by pointing at 6.7,
Group VII, where reactivity runs the opposite way.

## Objective

The student can name the alkali metals and say they are Group I, with one
outer-shell electron, and why that makes them react alike; say only Li, Na and K
are used in the school lab; give the physical properties (soft, low density, low
melting point) and read the book's table as **trends**, finding the value that
does not fit (potassium's density); use density = mass ÷ volume; say what is
seen when Li, Na and K meet water; give the products (the hydroxide — an alkali,
hence the name — and hydrogen) and the indicator change; give the reactions with
chlorine (chlorides) and oxygen (oxides that dissolve to give alkalis); state that
reactivity increases down the group and why they are stored under oil; predict
rubidium and caesium. **Extended:** why they are so reactive (lose one electron
to reach a full shell, forming 1+ ions; white ionic solids, colourless
solutions) and why reactivity increases down the group (the outer electron is
further from the nucleus, pulled less, lost more easily).

**Core vs Extended.** The Checkup lists "say why elements in a group react in a
similar way" under **Core**, so slide 4 is not marked Extended; "why so reactive,
in terms of electron transfer" and "trends in reactivity in terms of electron
shells" are Extended (slides 18–19).

## The mistakes the unit is built around

| Mistake | Where it is caught |
|---|---|
| Treating the alkali metals as typical metals (hard, dense, high melting point) | deck sort a2, workbook f1, quiz 1 |
| Reading the period or proton number as the outer electrons | deck c1–c2, quiz 2 |
| Describing one value as a "trend"; missing the value that does not fit | deck c3, a3, c4, workbook p2 and c3 |
| Forgetting density decides floating (Rb sinks) | deck c5 and a8, workbook p1, quiz 7 |
| Confusing the oxide with the hydroxide; the gas as oxygen | deck c9, c12, workbook f3 and p3, quiz 3, Equations banks |
| Thinking hydrogen, or the metal itself, makes the water alkaline | deck c8, quiz 10 |
| Reactivity in density order, or "all equal because one outer electron" | deck a7, quiz 4 |
| "Bigger nucleus pulls harder" / "more outer electrons" as the reason for the trend | deck c11, workbook c2, quiz 6, Source Analysis 2, Questions sq3 |
| Metal ion charge wrong (1−, 2+, period number) | deck c10, workbook c1, quiz 5 and 8 |
| Formulae that ignore the 1+ charge (NaO, LiCl₂, KO, Na₂OH); H and Cl as single atoms | Equations banks, quiz 8 |

## Equations (`SYMBOL_EQ`, 8 items)

2Li + 2H₂O → 2LiOH + H₂ · 2K + 2H₂O → 2KOH + H₂ · 2Rb + 2H₂O → 2RbOH + H₂ ·
2Li + Cl₂ → 2LiCl · 2Cs + Cl₂ → 2CsCl · 4Li + O₂ → 2Li₂O · 4K + O₂ → 2K₂O ·
Na₂O + H₂O → 2NaOH. Every item passes `checkItem`. Sodium metal is left to the
deck (the book's example), and potassium + chlorine is left out because it is
the spread's own end-of-page question 7a. The `note` is shown as a hint while
the student works, so it points at the charges and never writes the answer.
