# 6.4 Making Salts: the methods — unit plan (IGCSE_CHEM / M06_4A)

Wolsey Hall Module 6, Topic Four (6.4), first half. Built from book spreads **11.6
"Making salts (part I)"** and **11.7 "Making salts (part II)"** (pages 138–141), with the
Chapter 11 Checkup (page 144) as the guide to what the exam asks and which parts are
Extended. English-only (`bilingual: false`), so no `vn*` twins.

Stops before 11.8 (finding a concentration by titration), which is `M06_4B`. Here a
titration is only a **method for making a soluble salt from an alkali**: why it is
needed, the steps, the indicator, and why the run is repeated without it. No moles.

## Objective

The student can give the steps for making a salt from an acid and **an excess of a metal**
(add excess, filter, evaporate to a saturated solution, cool to crystallise), say which
metals that works for (Mg, Al, Zn, Fe — not Na, K, Ca, too violent; not Cu, Ag, Au, no
reaction; not Pb, too slow); do the same from **an excess of an insoluble base or
carbonate** (CuO / CuCO₃ + H₂SO₄ → blue CuSO₄, crystals of CuSO₄·5H₂O), and explain why
the solid is in excess and why it is filtered; explain why an **alkali** needs a
**titration** first (both reactants dissolve, so no excess can be filtered off), give its
steps with thymolphthalein, and why the second run has no indicator; use the
**solubility rules**; define **hydrated** and **anhydrous**. **Extended:** make an
insoluble salt by **precipitation** (mix, filter, rinse with distilled water, dry in a
warm oven), choose the two starting solutions, write the ionic equation and name the
spectator ions, give uses of precipitation in tests and industry, and define **water of
crystallisation**.

Extended follows the book's margin bar and the Checkup's Extended list: the whole
precipitation section, choosing the starting compounds, lab tests, and the term water
of crystallisation. Hydrated / anhydrous is Core.

## The mistakes the unit is built around

| Mistake | Where it is caught |
|---|---|
| Evaporating before filtering / filtering before the reaction is over | deck order a2, Order It, quiz 1 |
| Not adding enough solid, so acid is left in the salt | deck c2, c4; workbook f1; Questions sq1 |
| Reading "the solution turned blue" as "the acid is used up" | deck c3, Source Analysis 1, quiz 3 |
| Using copper (no reaction) or potassium / sodium / calcium (violent) with an acid | deck predict a3 and sort a4, workbook f4, quiz 2 and 10, Questions sq2 |
| Filtering off an excess that dissolves (an alkali, sodium carbonate) | deck c5, workbook p2, quiz 10, Questions sq3 |
| Confusing the burette with the pipette; thymolphthalein's colours | deck c6, workbook p1, quiz 4 |
| Leaving the indicator in the salt | deck c7, Order It, Questions sq3 |
| Forgetting an exception in the solubility rules (lead nitrate is soluble; calcium sulfate is not) | deck sort a5, workbook f3, quiz 5, Source Analysis 2 |
| Precipitating from an insoluble starting compound, or "precipitating" a soluble salt | deck c10, workbook p5 and c1, quiz 6 and 9, Source Analysis 2 |
| Keeping a spectator ion, or writing the precipitate as (aq) | deck sort a7, workbook c2, quiz 7, Spectator Strike |
| Counting hydrogen atoms instead of water molecules in ·6H₂O | deck c13, workbook c3 |

## The deck (22 slides)

1 opener (salt water on a windowsill) · 2 recap: what acids turn into salts (c1) ·
3 **method 1**, Zn + H₂SO₄, "excess", `METAL_METHOD` (c2) · 4 English: residue and
filtrate (**hotspot a1** on `FILTER_PARTS`: tap the residue) · 5 evaporate to saturated,
then cool (**order a2**: the zinc sulfate method) · 6 **predict a3**: copper foil in acid ·
7 which metals (**sort a4**: can method 1 make this salt?) · 8 **method 2**, CuO + H₂SO₄
(and CuCO₃), `BASE_METHOD` (c3) · 9 why excess, why filter (c4) · 10 **method 3**: both
reactants dissolve, so titrate first (c5) · 11 the titration, thymolphthalein,
`TITRATION_RIG` (c6) · 12 repeat without the indicator, evaporate (c7) · 13 the
solubility rules, `SOLUBILITY_TABLE` (**sort a5**: soluble or insoluble) · **Extended:**
14 precipitation, BaCl₂ + MgSO₄, `PRECIP_PARTICLES` (c8) · 15 the steps, `PRECIP_METHOD`
(c9) · 16 choosing the two solutions (c10) · 17 which method? (**sort a6**: excess solid /
titration / precipitation) · **Extended:** 18 the ionic equation, `PRECIP_STRIKE`
(**sort a7**: AgNO₃ + KBr, takes part or spectator) · 19 precipitation at work — tests,
waste water, pigments and film (c11) · 20 hydrated and anhydrous, `HYDRATED` (c12) ·
**Extended:** 21 water of crystallisation (c13) · 22 countable recap (c14).

The "which method" sort has three bins, not four: no salt can ONLY be made from its
metal (the oxide or carbonate always works too), so "metal" and "insoluble base" are one
bin, "excess solid, then filter".

The ionic-equation slide uses M06_2's words exactly: **split into ions**, **stay
whole**, **strike out the spectator ions**, **what is left is the ionic equation**. The
deck's precipitations (BaCl₂ + MgSO₄, the book's; AgNO₃ + KBr) are not in the Spectator
Strike pool.
