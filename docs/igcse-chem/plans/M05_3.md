# M05_3 Measuring the Rate of a Reaction — lesson & unit plan (IGCSE_CHEM)

Module 5, topic 3 of the **IGCSE Chemistry** track (`IGCSE_CHEM`), built from
*Complete Chemistry for Cambridge IGCSE* spreads **9.1 Introducing reaction rates**
and **9.2 Measuring the rate of a reaction** (book pages 102–105), plus the
loss-of-mass method from **9.4** and the Chapter 9 Checkup (page 116–117).
English only (`bilingual: false`), so there are no `vn*` twins. The track's exemplar
for a **graph and practical** unit.

## Objective

The student can say what **rate** means and define the **rate of a reaction** both
ways (reactant used up / product formed per unit time); choose what to measure and
say why a **gas** is easiest; label the **gas-syringe** apparatus and give the reason
for every step (clean the ribbon, acid in **excess**, stopper in at once, clock at the
same moment, regular intervals); describe the **loss-of-mass** method and why it fails
for hydrogen; read a volume–time curve (**up from the time, across to the volume**;
steep → less steep → flat); work out **the minute's rate = end reading − start
reading** and the **average rate = total ÷ time taken** with the right **unit**; say
in one sentence why the rate falls; and name a **source of error** with its effect.
It stops before *changing* the rate (9.3–9.4), which the checklist hands to M05_4.

## The mistakes the unit is built around

| Mistake | Where it is caught |
|---|---|
| Reading the running total as the rate in a minute (58 instead of 58 − 53) | notes c4, c8 · Practice p2 · Quiz a2, a8 |
| Dividing by the time you stopped watching, not when it went flat | notes c6, c9 · Practice p3, p4 · Quiz a6 |
| Time ÷ volume, or multiplying (min/cm³, 144 cm³/min) | notes c9 · Practice p4, c3 · Quiz a6, a10 |
| "Ends higher = faster" (height is amount, steepness is speed) | notes c14 · Source Analysis diag_2 |
| Taking the flat part as "fastest" | notes c7 · Quiz a4 |
| A unit with the wrong quantity or no time in it (cm³, g/s for a volume) | notes c10 · Practice f4 · Quiz a3 |
| A total with no time counted as a rate | notes a2 (sort), c2 · Quiz a1 |
| Thinking the excess acid runs out / decides the total | Practice f3 · Quiz a5, a7 |
| Naming an error without its effect | notes slide 21, c13 · Practice c1 · Quiz a9 · Questions sq5 |

## Items

**Notes (20 scored).** Checks c1–c14 (rate; rate of reaction; why the gas; syringe
reading is a total; stopper in at once; when it finished; flat curve; fifth-minute
rate 58 − 53 = 5; average 36 ÷ 4 = 9; unit g/s; what the cotton wool is for;
balance poor for hydrogen; late stopper; steeper = faster). Activities: **a1 order** fastest → slowest (firework,
match, cake, sour milk, rust) · **a2 sort** reactant used / product formed / not a
rate · **a3 hotspot** tap the gas syringe · **a4 order** the six method steps ·
**a5 predict** the rate falls · **a6 estimate** volume at 1.5 min (28 cm³, ±10%).
The book's magnesium results (14, 25, 33 … 40 cm³; 40 ÷ 5 = 8) are taught on
slides 16–17.

**Practice (12).** Focus: f1 definition · f2 apparatus (gas syringe, stopclock) ·
f3 excess · f4 rate units vs not. Practice: p1 step → reason · p2 third minute
37 − 28 = 9 · p3 finished at 6 min, 48 cm³ · p4 average 48 ÷ 6 = 8 · p5 which
reaction gives a gas. Challenge: c1 which error (oxide coat) · c2 loss of mass,
cotton wool, why not hydrogen · c3 average loss of mass 1.00 ÷ 5 = 0.20 g/min.

**Questions (5 × 3 marks).** sq1 describe the gas-syringe method (zinc + sulfuric
acid) · sq2 explain the hydrogen peroxide curve (fast, slower, stops) · sq3 second
minute 52 − 30 = 22 and average 80 ÷ 5 = 16 cm³/min · sq4 why the balance falls and
why cotton wool · sq5 one error (calcium + water), its effect, an improvement.

**Source Analysis (3).** diag_1 MCQ why the balance falls · diag_2 MCQ A faster, B
more gas · diag_3 written: B over at 8 min, 48 cm³, 48 ÷ 8 = 6 cm³/min.

**Quiz (10 MCQ, key A×3 B×3 C×2 D×2).** a1 not a rate measure · a2 second-minute
gas 42 − 24 = 18 · a3 cm³/s · a4 curve shape · a5 why it slows · a6 90 ÷ 3 = 30 ·
a7 which reactant decides the total · a8 second-minute mass 85.10 − 84.85 = 0.25 g ·
a9 late clock → average too high · a10 first minute vs average.

Graph reading is left to Rate Reader: the Practice set reads a table, and only Quiz
a4 is about a curve's shape.

## Rate Reader items

Five experiments in `rateGraph.js`, none of them the book's magnesium results. Every
answer is derived from the plotted points by `src/utils/rateCurve.js`.

| Item | Experiment | Axes | Asks | Why it is here |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `rg_zinc` | zinc + excess sulfuric acid | min · cm³ | read ×2, timeFor, end, total, why | plain readings, both directions |
| `rg_peroxide` | hydrogen peroxide decomposing | min · cm³ | interval ×3, steepest, average | the rate in ONE minute; first use of the unit |
| `rg_marble` | excess marble + hydrochloric acid | s · cm³ | interval ×2, timeFor, average, why | a 20 s interval, so the difference must be divided |
| `rg_balance` | marble + acid on a balance | min · g | read, interval, end, total, average | loss of mass; decimals; g/min |
| `rg_two_metals` | metals X and Y in excess acid | s · cm³ | compare ×2, end ×2, average ×2 | steeper is faster, higher is more |

Average rates come out clean by design: 10, 14.4, 0.6, 0.4, 1.5 and 0.5.
