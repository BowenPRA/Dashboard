# 6.7 Group 7: The Halogens — unit plan (IGCSE_CHEM / M06_7)

Wolsey Hall Module 6, Topic Seven (6.7). Built from book spread **12.3 "Group VII:
the halogens"** (pages 150–151), with the reactivity explanation from **12.4**
(page 152: point 4 on shells, point 6 on density, the ion-charge row) and the
Group I contrast from **12.2**. The Chapter 12 Checkup (pages 158–159) is the
guide to what the exam asks. An **equations unit** on the M06_2 exemplar.
English-only (`bilingual: false`), so no `vn*` twins.

**Two names, one group.** Wolsey writes "Group 7", the book "Group VII". Slide 2
says once that they are the same; the chemistry then says Group VII.

## Objective

The student can say that the halogens are non-metals with coloured, poisonous,
diatomic molecules; describe chlorine (pale yellow-green gas), bromine
(red-brown liquid) and iodine (grey-black solid); give the trends down the group
(boiling point and density increase, reactivity **decreases** — the opposite of
Group I); describe hot iron wool in each halogen and name the iron(III) halides;
say that halogens make ionic halides with metals and covalent molecules with
non-metals; predict and describe **displacement** ("a halogen displaces a less
reactive halogen from a solution of its halide") and write its symbol equation;
say why the group reacts alike (7 outer-shell electrons). **Extended:** explain
why halogens are so reactive (one electron to gain; halide ions are 1−), why
reactivity falls down the group (outer shell further from the nucleus), predict
fluorine and astatine, and write the ionic equation of a displacement.

## The mistakes the unit is built around

| Mistake | Where it is caught |
|---|---|
| Carrying the Group I trend into Group VII ("lower is more reactive") | deck c5, order a5, workbook p5, quiz 3 and 8 |
| Writing a halogen as single atoms (Cl, Br) | deck c2 and c11, Equations banks, quiz 5 |
| Running a displacement backwards / thinking any halogen displaces any halide | deck predict a8, sort a9, c9, workbook p3 and c3, quiz 4 and 5, Questions sq1 |
| Halide formulae that ignore the charges (NaBr₂, FeCl₂ for iron(III), CaBr) | Equations banks, workbook c2, quiz 5 |
| A halide ion as 1+, or as 7− | deck c7, workbook c2, quiz 7 |
| Splitting a dissolved halogen into ions / keeping the metal ion in the ionic equation | deck sort a10, quiz 9, Spectator Strike |
| Mixing up the colours: bromine (orange) vs iodine (red-brown) in solution | deck a2, c8, quiz 4 |
| "More shells = more pull" | deck c10, workbook c1, Questions sq2, Source Analysis 3 |

"Why do they react in a similar way?" (slide 12) is **Core**: the Checkup lists it
under Core. The electron-transfer reasons (13, 21) and the ionic equation (20)
carry `eyebrow: 'Extended'`.

Slide 20 uses M06_2's words exactly — **split into ions**, **stay whole**,
**strike out the spectator ions**, **what is left is the ionic equation** — and
adds the one new point for this unit: a dissolved halogen, Cl₂(aq), is molecules
and stays whole, so "(aq)" alone does not decide.

## Equations (`SYMBOL_EQ`, 8 items)

2Na + Br₂ → 2NaBr · H₂ + Cl₂ → 2HCl · H₂ + I₂ → 2HI · 2Fe + 3Cl₂ → 2FeCl₃ ·
2Fe + 3Br₂ → 2FeBr₃ · Cl₂ + 2NaBr → 2NaCl + Br₂ · Br₂ + 2KI → 2KBr + I₂ ·
Cl₂ + 2NaI → 2NaCl + I₂. Every item passes `checkItem`. Hydrogen + **iodine**
stands in for hydrogen + bromine, whose symbol equation is Checkup question 4c.
FeI₃ is taught in the deck (the book gives it) but not set as an item.
