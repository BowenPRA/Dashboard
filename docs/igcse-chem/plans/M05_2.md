## Objective

The student can state the unit of energy and convert **kJ ↔ J**; define **bond
energy** and give its unit, **kJ/mol**; read a **bond energy table** and say which
bond is stronger; write an equation out as **displayed formulae** and **count the
bonds, remembering the coefficient**; total the **energy in** (bonds broken) and the
**energy out** (bonds made); calculate **ΔH = energy in − energy out** and say what its
**sign** means; read and draw the energy diagram with the **"bonds broken" level at
the top**; and explain why **N≡N makes nitrogen inert**.

## The mistakes the unit is built around

Every distractor in the deck, the workbook and the quiz is one of these:

1. **Forgetting the coefficient** — 2NH₃ has 6 N–H bonds, not 3.
2. **Out − in** — the right size with the wrong sign.
3. **Adding the totals** instead of subtracting them.
4. **Dropping or flipping the sign** — "92" instead of "+92"; minus read as endothermic.
5. **Splitting a double bond** — O=O counted as two O–O bonds instead of one entry of 498.
6. **Reading the wrong bond** — O–H for O=O, C–O for C=O, H–I for I–I.
7. **Per equation vs per mole** — the ΔH of an equation is for the moles it shows.
8. **J vs kJ** — multiplying when you should divide.

## The method (deck = engine, same names, same order)

1. Show all the bonds (displayed formulae).
2. Count each type of bond broken — the coefficient counts.
3. Multiply by the bond energies and add: **energy in**.
4. The same for the bonds made: **energy out**.
5. **ΔH = energy in − energy out.**
6. Read the sign: minus = exothermic, plus = endothermic.
