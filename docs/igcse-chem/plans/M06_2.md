# 6.2 Reactions of Acids and Bases — unit plan (IGCSE_CHEM / M06_2)

Wolsey Hall Module 6, Topic Two (6.2). Built from book spreads **11.3 "The
reactions of acids and bases"** and **11.4 "A closer look at neutralisation"**
(pages 132–135), with the Chapter 11 Checkup (page 144) as the guide to what the
exam asks. The track's **equations exemplar**: word → symbol → ionic, with two
engines side by side. English-only (`bilingual: false`), so no `vn*` twins.

Stops before 6.3 (acidic, basic and amphoteric oxides) and 6.4 (the practical
methods for making salts, solubility rules, titration).

## Objective

The student can name the salt an acid makes (chlorides, sulfates, nitrates);
give the three typical acid reactions — with a **metal**, a **base** (alkali or
insoluble) and a **carbonate** — as general word equations and as balanced
symbol equations with state symbols; name each gas and its test; give the two
typical reactions of bases (only salt and water with an acid; ammonia from an
ammonium salt); define **neutralisation** and say why acid + metal is not one;
explain where the water comes from (H⁺ + OH⁻ → H₂O); give uses of neutralisation
outside the lab. **Extended:** write an ionic equation in the book's three steps,
name the spectator ions, write the insoluble-base case 2H⁺(aq) + O²⁻(s) → H₂O(l),
and explain acids as **proton donors** and bases as **proton acceptors**.

## The mistakes the unit is built around

| Mistake | Where it is caught |
|---|---|
| Calling acid + metal a neutralisation ("the acid is used up") | deck sort a4, workbook p3, quiz 3, Source Analysis 1, Questions sq2 |
| Giving hydrogen for a carbonate (every fizz is hydrogen) | deck predict a3, check c5, workbook f2, quiz 2 and 10 |
| Naming the salt after the wrong acid, or keeping "carbonate"/"hydroxide" in the name | deck sort a1, workbook f1 and p2, quiz 1 |
| Choosing a carbonate or a metal when "salt and water only" is wanted; using copper metal | deck c13, workbook p1 and c2 |
| Keeping a spectator ion in the ionic equation | deck a6, workbook c1, quiz 5 and 9, Spectator Strike |
| Splitting water (or a gas, a solid, a metal) into ions | deck a5 and c9, quiz 5 |
| Reversing donor and acceptor | deck c11–c12, workbook p4 and c3, quiz 7 and 8, Source Analysis 2 |
| Salt formulae that ignore the charges (KSO₄, CaNO₃), H for H₂, HO for H₂O | Equations banks |

The ionic-equation slides use the engine's words exactly: **split into ions**,
**stay whole**, **strike out the spectator ions**, **what is left is the ionic
equation**. Slide 19 says outright that the oxide is the one solid written as an
ion, (s), which is how the book writes it and how the engine's `split: true`
item behaves. The deck's own ionic examples (H₂SO₄ / NaOH / MgCl₂ in a5, NaOH +
HNO₃ in a6) avoid the engine's seven reactions.

**Sized for 1280×720.** A split slide's text column is ~400px wide beside its

## Equations (`SYMBOL_EQ`, 8 items, all fresh)

Zn + 2HCl → ZnCl₂ + H₂ · Fe + H₂SO₄ → FeSO₄ + H₂ · 2KOH + H₂SO₄ → K₂SO₄ + 2H₂O ·
Ca(OH)₂ + 2HNO₃ → Ca(NO₃)₂ + 2H₂O · ZnO + 2HNO₃ → Zn(NO₃)₂ + H₂O ·
Na₂CO₃ + 2HCl → 2NaCl + H₂O + CO₂ · CuCO₃ + H₂SO₄ → CuSO₄ + H₂O + CO₂ ·
2NaOH + (NH₄)₂SO₄ → Na₂SO₄ + 2H₂O + 2NH₃. Every item passes `checkItem`. Nitric
acid is never set with a metal (it does not give hydrogen), and copper only
appears as a compound.

## Spectator Strike items

Seven reactions in `ionicEq.js`, none of them the book's HCl + NaOH or MgO + HCl. The
ions, the spectators, the ionic equation and the kind of reaction are derived by
`src/utils/ionicEquation.js`; the `ionic` line on each item is a check.

| Item | Reaction | Spectators | Ionic equation | Kind |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `ie_koh_hno3` | HNO₃ + KOH | K⁺, NO₃⁻ | H⁺ + OH⁻ → H₂O | neutralisation |
| `ie_naoh_h2so4` | H₂SO₄ + 2NaOH | Na⁺, SO₄²⁻ | H⁺ + OH⁻ → H₂O (÷2) | neutralisation |
| `ie_zn_hcl` | Zn + 2HCl | Cl⁻ | Zn + 2H⁺ → Zn²⁺ + H₂ | acid + metal |
| `ie_caoh2_hcl` | Ca(OH)₂ + 2HCl | Ca²⁺, Cl⁻ | H⁺ + OH⁻ → H₂O (÷2) | neutralisation |
| `ie_k2co3_hno3` | K₂CO₃ + 2HNO₃ | K⁺, NO₃⁻ | 2H⁺ + CO₃²⁻ → H₂O + CO₂ | acid + carbonate |
| `ie_fe_h2so4` | Fe + H₂SO₄ | SO₄²⁻ | Fe + 2H⁺ → Fe²⁺ + H₂ | acid + metal |
| `ie_cuo_h2so4` | CuO(s) + H₂SO₄ | Cu²⁺, SO₄²⁻ | 2H⁺ + O²⁻ → H₂O | neutralisation |

The last item follows the book's way of writing an insoluble base (`split: true`).

neutralisation (D) · 4 spectator ion defined (B) · 5 ionic equation, H₂SO₄ + KOH

(D). Key: A3 B2 C2 D3. Two items ask for a whole ionic equation.
