# The GED Sprint — Mapping the Modules for a 4–6 Week Independent Run

A student studying alone, a few hours a day, for four to six weeks, then sitting the GED.
This is the map: what the test actually demands, what we have, what to build, in what
order, and what a day looks like.

**Assumptions.** One Vietnamese ESL student, ~3 hours a day, 6 days a week, all four
subject tests, nothing booked yet. If they are only sitting one or two subjects, the plan
is modular by subject — drop the rows and reallocate the hours.

**Risk order, from the teacher who knows the student:**

> **English ≫ Social Studies ≫ Math > Science**

Everything below is sequenced against that, not against what is easiest to build.

---

## 1. The budget, honestly

3 h/day × 6 days × 5 weeks ≈ **90 hours of student time.**

Ninety hours is not enough to *teach* four subjects from scratch. It is enough to **teach
the skills that transfer, drill the formats, and close the specific gaps a diagnostic
finds.** Every decision below follows from that.

The build budget matters just as much: you are authoring content *while* the student
studies. Realistically that is 2–3 new units a week. Plan for ~12 new units, not 25 — §6 is
the triage list if only ten get made.

---

## 2. What the test actually is

Four separate tests, **145 to pass each**, taken on separate days if you want.

| Test | Time | Content weights | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Reasoning Through Language Arts** | 150 min (incl. 10-min break) | Reading ~75% informational / 25% literary; language & editing in context | **45-min Extended Response** essay from two opposing sources |
| **Mathematical Reasoning** | 115 min (3-min break) | **Quantitative 45%**, **Algebraic 55%** | Formula sheet provided; TI-30XS calculator on part 2 |
| **Science** | 90 min, no break | **Life 40%, Physical 40%, Earth & Space 20%** | No essay. Mostly reading passages, charts and experiments |
| **Social Studies** | 70 min, no break | **Civics & Government 50%**, US History 20%, Geography & World 15%, Economics 15% | No essay. Heavy on documents, cartoons, maps, data |

Two things in that table should change the plan on their own:

- **Science and Social Studies are reading tests wearing a lab coat.** The bulk of the
  items give you the information and ask you to reason about it. Content knowledge is a
  thin vocabulary layer on top of a reading skill.
- **Civics & Government is half the Social Studies test**, and we have built zero of it.
  Both existing units are US History — 20% of that test.

## 3. Reading the risk order

### English is not one worry, it is four

If English is the top worry, it is not a subject that needs more hours — it is a **tax on
every other test**. Ranking the four by how much English stands between the student and the
marks:

| Test | English load | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **RLA** | Highest | The whole test *is* English, and 45 minutes of it is producing English under time |
| **Social Studies** | Very high | Founding documents, 18th-century constitutional prose, political cartoons carrying idiom and irony. Arguably the hardest *reading* on the GED |
| **Science** | Moderate–high | Dense passages, but technical vocabulary is Latinate and precisely defined — often easier for an ESL reader than idiom — and charts carry a lot of the meaning |
| **Math** | Lowest | Symbols are universal, a formula sheet is provided, and roughly half the items are procedural with little text |

Your order matches this almost exactly. **The one I would double-check is Science.** Not
because the content is hard — because it is 90 minutes of English passages and we have
**zero units built**. "Least worried" is a judgement about the student; "least prepared" is
a fact about the repo, and those two are pointing in opposite directions.

### What is fixable in five weeks, and what is not

This is the single most important paragraph in the plan.

**Not fixable in five weeks:** general reading fluency, vocabulary breadth, speed of
comprehension. These move on a scale of months. Do not spend the sprint trying.

**Highly fixable in five weeks:**
- **The editing strand of RLA** — fragments, run-ons, comma splices, agreement, tense,
  punctuation. These are *rules*, they are finite, and they are drillable to automaticity.
- **The Extended Response** — a fixed structure plus a bank of sentence frames. It is a
  formula, not an act of creativity.
- **Question-stem language** — "which statement best summarises", "the author implies",
  "which piece of evidence most strongly supports". A student who loses marks decoding the
  *question* is losing marks they already had.
- **Test-day process** — timing per item, when to skip, how to use the passage.

So the English hours go into rules, templates and stem vocabulary — **not** into "read
more." That is what makes a five-week English intervention possible at all.

### Four calls

**Call 1 — Teach reading once, cash it three times.** `ENG_1A` (main idea & detail),
`ENG_1B` (purpose, tone, point of view) and `ENG_1C` (claims, evidence, arguments) are
already built and are **also** the Science and Social Studies skill set. Build one applied
unit per subject that runs those same skills over science data and social-studies
documents. Three subjects to teach becomes one skill plus two vocabulary layers — and given
the risk order, every hour spent on reading is being spent on the top two worries at once.

**Call 2 — Sit them in ascending order of worry.** The GED lets you book subjects
separately. Give the biggest worry the longest runway:

> **Math → Science → Social Studies → RLA**

Math first: it is the least English-dependent test, it has two units built plus three cheap
reskins available, and an early pass on the board is worth a great deal to a student who is
frightened of the English ones. RLA last, with five weeks of daily writing behind it.

**Call 3 — Build a shared test-language vocabulary bank.** Not general English. The ~250
words that actually appear in GED *stems and passages* across all four tests: *infer,
imply, cite, evaluate, summarise, contradict, primary source, amendment, ratify, hypothesis,
variable, coefficient, proportion*. One bank, drawn on by the Review deck in every track.
Cheap to build, and it attacks the top worry from underneath every subject.

**Call 4 — Let GED Ready make the go/no-go decision.** The official practice test is the
only calibrated instrument available. Use it twice per subject: **once early as a
diagnostic**, and **once ~4–5 days before booking the real test.** Green "likely to pass"
is the trigger to book. Do the first diagnostics in **week 1, before authoring anything
new** — one afternoon of diagnostics beats a week of guessing at gaps.

## 4. The module map

### 4.1 What already exists — including in places you may not be looking

| Track | Built | Reusable for the GED |
|---|---|---|
| `GED_ENG` | `ENG_0A` pronouns · `ENG_0B` subject–verb · `ENG_1A` main idea · `ENG_1B` purpose/tone · `ENG_1C` claims & evidence | All five, directly. `1A/1B/1C` also carry Science and Social Studies |
| `GED_MATH` | `MATH_1A` expressions & equations · `MATH_1B` linear equations & inequalities | Both, directly |
| `GED_HISTORY` | `HIST_1A` colonial/revolution · `HIST_1B` independence & constitution | Yes — and `1B` is partly civics; mine it |
| `Y9` | `MATH_1A` correlation & scatter graphs · `MATH_2A` solids, volume & surface area · `SCIENCE_2A` tectonics | **Scatter graphs → GED data & statistics. Volume/surface area → GED geometric measurement. Tectonics → GED Earth Science.** Reskin, don't rewrite |
| `Y8` | `MATH_1A` parallel lines & angles · `SCIENCE_1A` light & colour | Angles → geometry; light → GED physical science (waves) |
| `Y7_MATH` | `U01_1` adding & subtracting integers | → GED number sense. Also the best-polished lesson in the repo |

Six units' worth of GED-relevant material is sitting outside the GED folders. **Reskinning
is roughly a quarter the cost of authoring** — and with English and Social Studies taking
the hours, the maths build has to come cheap.

### 4.2 GED_ENG — 5 of 10 built, and now the priority track

Blueprint exists in [ged-english-lessons.md](ged-english-lessons.md). Remaining, reordered
for the risk profile:

| # | Unit | Why this priority |
|---|---|---|
| **10** | **Extended Response capstone** | ~20% of the RLA score, and it is a formula. Highest marginal return of anything in the app |
| **4** | Sentence boundaries (fragments, run-ons, comma splices) | The commonest ESL error and a high item count. Pure rules |
| **5** | Punctuation & confusables (commas, apostrophes, their/there/they're) | High item count, learnable in a session |
| **3** | Verb tense & consistency | Tense is a known Vietnamese-L1 difficulty — promote it above "if time" |
| 6 | Transitions & organization | Largely absorbed by the essay capstone; build only if the diagnostic asks |

**Plus a non-unit, high-value addition: the essay sentence-frame bank.** Roughly fifteen
frames the student drills to automaticity — *"The author of Source A claims that ___.
However, this evidence is weak because ___."* Frames are the highest-leverage ESL writing
intervention there is: they remove the sentence-construction load so the 45 minutes go on
thinking, not on grammar.

### 4.3 GED_HISTORY — worry #2, and built the wrong 20%

| Id | Unit | Weight served |
|---|---|---|
| `HIST_0A` | **Reading social studies sources: documents, political cartoons, maps, charts** | All items — and this is the hardest English on the test. Build first |
| `HIST_2A` | **Civics 1: the Constitution, three branches, checks & balances, federalism** | **50%** |
| `HIST_2B` | **Civics 2: rights & amendments, elections, the role of the citizen** | **50%** |
| `HIST_3A` | Economics: supply & demand, markets, money, incentives | 15% |
| `HIST_1A` / `HIST_1B` | US history | ✅ built — covers most of the 20% |

Geography (15%) folds into `HIST_0A` as map-reading rather than getting its own unit.

Civics is the ideal worry-#2 target: it is **50% of the test, finite, factual, and fast to
learn** — the branches of government do not change. It is the cheapest large block of marks
anywhere in this plan.

| Id | Unit | Status |
|---|---|---|

formula sheet. A student who has never used either loses marks to the interface, not the
maths. Half an hour, twice. **Given maths is worry #3, this is the cheapest maths win
available** — take it before authoring another unit.

## 5. The daily shape

Three hours. **English is not one of the two subjects — it is the medium.** Every block is
also English practice: read the passage aloud, log unknown words, answer in full sentences.

| Time | Block | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:15 | **Review** | Mixed recall from finished units — previous days' misses first |
| 0:15–1:15 | **English** | The primary block, every day. Editing rules, reading strand, or essay work |
| 1:15–1:30 | Break | Real break, away from the screen |
| 1:30–2:15 | **Rotating subject** | Social Studies ×3 days, Math ×2, Science ×1 per week |
| 2:15–2:45 | **Writing** | One short answer, or a timed essay paragraph. Every single day |
| 2:45–3:00 | **Test-language vocabulary** | Stem words and subject terms + yesterday's misses |

**Weekly:** six study days, one **fully off** — protect it; five weeks is long enough to
burn out in. One day (make it Saturday), replace the English block with a **full timed
section under test conditions**: phone away, no Vietnamese, clock running.

**The writing block is not optional and not negotiable.** Thirty minutes a day for five
weeks is ~12 hours of English production. Against this risk order it is the highest-value
half hour on the timetable.

### Hour allocation across the sprint (~90 h)

Reweighted from the original plan to follow the risk order.

| Subject | Hours | Share | Change | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **RLA / English** | ~32 | 36% | ▲ from 24 | Top worry, and it taxes every other test |
| **Social Studies** | ~20 | 22% | ▲ from 12 | Worry #2; 50% of it is unbuilt civics; hardest reading on the GED |
| **Mathematical Reasoning** | ~18 | 20% | ▼ from 28 | Worry #3, lowest English load, and three units come cheap as reskins |
| **Science** | ~10 | 11% | ▼ from 14 | Lowest worry; rides almost entirely on the reading strand |
| **Mixed review + timed practice** | ~10 | 11% | — | Non-negotiable — this is the retention |

### Vietnamese: fade it on a schedule

With English as the top worry the instinct is to lean harder on Vietnamese. That is the
wrong direction for the back half, because the test has none.

- **Weeks 1–2:** full bilingual everywhere. Understanding first.
- **Week 3:** glossary-only in practice tasks; Vietnamese still available in explanations.
- **Weeks 4–6:** **all timed practice is English-only.** Vietnamese comes back on the
  review screen afterwards, where it does its actual job — explaining the miss.

## 6. If only ten units get built

Ranked by marks-per-hour-of-authoring **against this risk order**. Build in order and stop
when the sprint does.

1. `ENG_10` **Extended Response capstone** — ~20% of RLA, and it is a formula
2. `ENG_4` **Sentence boundaries** — commonest ESL error, high item count, pure rules
3. `HIST_2A` **Civics 1** — 50% of a test at zero coverage, and fast to learn
4. `HIST_0A` **Reading social studies sources** — the hardest English on the GED
5. `ENG_5` **Punctuation & confusables** — a session's teaching, a lot of items
6. `HIST_2B` **Civics 2**
7. `SCI_0A` **Reading science** — one unit that serves a whole test
8. `MATH_1E` **Word problems → equations** — where the English tax hits maths
9. `MATH_0D` Data & statistics — *reskin of `Y9/MATH_1A`, cheap*
10. `ENG_3` Verb tense & consistency — *a known Vietnamese-L1 difficulty*

Non-unit items that outrank most of this list for cost-to-value, and should be slotted in
wherever there is a spare afternoon: **the essay sentence-frame bank**, **the test-language
vocabulary bank**, and the **calculator + formula-sheet session**.

`MATH_0E` (geometry reskin) and `MATH_0A` (integers reskin) are cheap enough to take
opportunistically if a week runs light.

## 7. What to change in the app — and what to leave alone

The [renderer work](lesson-renderer-gap.md) and the full
[independent-learning](independent-learning.md) programme are a **next-term project**. In a
five-week sprint, content and retrieval beat polish. Concretely:

**Do now**
- **Wave 1 + Wave 2 from [independent-learning.md](independent-learning.md)** — progress
  timestamps and a per-item log, then the 15-minute Review deck. This is the daily
  0:00–0:15 block; without it that block does not exist.
- **A shared vocabulary bank** the Review deck can draw from across tracks — the vehicle
  for Call 3.
- **A timed mixed-practice mode** — reuse `Assessment` with items pooled across a whole
  track. This is the Saturday block.
- **An English-only toggle for timed practice**, so the week-3 scaffold fade is enforced by
  the app rather than by willpower.
- **Fix `Recognition` to actually test** — it currently scores a point for either button
  ([Recognition.jsx:20](../src/tasks/Recognition.jsx#L20)), so vocabulary is never checked.
  With English as the top worry, an untested vocabulary task is the worst bug in the app.
- **Drop `SPELLING` from `GED_MATH` units** — busy work
  ([ged-english-lessons.md](ged-english-lessons.md) §3), and every hour is spoken for.
- **Create the `GED_SCIENCE` data folder** — the track is registered
  ([trackRegistry.js:37](../src/components/trackRegistry.js#L37)) pointing at nothing.

**Leave alone until after the test**
- New Notes layouts, note-card tones, the imagery pipeline, bilingual widgets.
- The English-improvement trend chart and error tagging.
- Any rewrite of already-built units that a diagnostic has not flagged.

## 8. The module grid

**One module per weekday.** A module is 100 XP, about three hours — see
[ged-unit-shape.md](ged-unit-shape.md) §2. Saturday is review and one timed rehearsal;
Sunday is off. So the sprint is five modules a week, and the whole thing is a 25-cell grid.

`✅` = built today · `♻` = reskin of an existing Y7–Y9 unit (~quarter the cost) ·
**bold** = new build

| | Mon | Tue | Wed | Thu | Fri |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **W1** | `ENG_0A` Pronouns ✅ | `ENG_0B` Subject–Verb ✅ | `MATH_1A` Expressions & Equations ✅ | `ENG_1A` Main Idea & Detail ✅ | `HIST_1A` Colonial America ✅ |
| **W2** | `ENG_1B` Purpose & Tone ✅ | `MATH_1B` Linear Equations ✅ | `ENG_1C` Claims & Evidence ✅ | `HIST_1B` Independence & Constitution ✅ | `MATH_0D` **Data & Statistics** ♻ |
| **W3** | `ENG_4` **Sentence Boundaries** | `HIST_2A` **Civics 1** | `MATH_0E` **Geometric Measurement** ♻ | `ENG_5` **Punctuation & Confusables** | `HIST_0A` **Reading SS Sources** |
| **W4** | `ENG_10` **Extended Response** | `SCI_0A` **Reading Science** | `HIST_2B` **Civics 2** | `MATH_1E` **Word Problems → Equations** | `SCI_1A` **Life Science** |
| **W5** | `ENG_3` **Verb Tense** | `HIST_3A` **Economics** | `SCI_3A` **Earth & Space** ♻ | `MATH_0B` **Fractions, Decimals, Percents** | `ENG_6` **Transitions & Organization** |
| **W6** | *no new modules* | | | | |

**Week 6 is deliberately empty of new content** — it is timed rehearsal, error review and
the last two test sittings.

### What the grid tells you

| | Modules | Share | Matches the risk order? |
|---|---|---|---|
| English | 10 | 40% | Top worry ✔ |
| Social Studies | 6 | 24% | Worry #2 ✔ |
| Math | 6 | 24% | Worry #3 ✔ |
| Science | 3 | 12% | Lowest worry ✔ |

**9 modules exist. 16 need building.** Weeks 1 and 2 need almost nothing authored, which
buys the first fortnight to build weeks 3–5. From week 3 that is five modules a week, two of
which are reskins.

If the build falls behind, drop from the tail in this order — each is the least
marks-per-module in its subject: `ENG_6`, `MATH_0B`, `HIST_3A`, `SCI_1A`. Never drop
`ENG_10`, `HIST_2A` or `SCI_0A`.

### Where the tests sit

| Week | Test |
|---|---|
| W3 Fri | GED Ready · Math → book the real one |
| W4 | **Sit Mathematical Reasoning.** GED Ready · Science |
| W5 | **Sit Science.** GED Ready · Social Studies |
| W6 | **Sit Social Studies**, then **sit RLA** |

### Alongside the modules

These run every day and are not modules:

- **15 min Review** before the module — mixed recall from finished modules.
- **30 min writing** after it — one short answer or a timed essay paragraph, every day
  from day one. If a day runs short, cut the module's optional tail, not the writing.
- **Saturday:** one full timed section under test conditions. Phone away, clock running,
  English only from week 4.

**Diagnostics:** run GED Ready across the four subjects in the first weekend, *before*
Monday. It costs a few dollars a subject and it is the only calibrated read on where the
student actually is — it will move several modules around. Keep the RLA essay it produces
as the "before" writing sample.

### Four-week variant

Cut week 2 and week 5. Weeks 1 → 3 → 4 → 6 in the grid above, and move the Math sitting to
the end of week 2. Subject ratios hold; the tail-drops listed above go first.

---

## 9. The three things that will actually decide it

1. **Did the diagnostic happen in week 1?** Everything above is a prior. The diagnostic is
   the evidence, and with English this dominant it will likely move 15–20 hours somewhere I
   have not predicted.
2. **Did the writing block happen every day?** It is the only block that improves all four
   scores at once, and it is the easiest to skip.
3. **Did the Vietnamese actually fade?** If week 5 still reads the Vietnamese first, the
   student sits an English test having never rehearsed one.
