# Motors and Electromagnetic Induction — unit plan (OLEVEL_PHYS / C09_3)

Chapter 9, spreads **9.5 Magnetic force on a current** (pages 206–207), **9.6 Electric
motors** (pages 208–209) and **9.7 Electromagnetic induction** (pages 210–211). A
**two-way effect** unit: a current in a field feels a force (the motor effect), and a
conductor moved in a field has an e.m.f. induced in it (the generator effect) — the same
three things, field, current and motion, with a different one unknown. English-only
(`bilingual: false`).

Recaps `C09_2` in one slide. Stops before 9.8 (the a.c. generator).

## Objective

The student can state that a current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field feels a force
(none when parallel to the field, none on a copper wire with no current); state the three
factors that increase it (current, field strength, length of conductor in the field) and
the two changes that reverse it; apply **Fleming's left-hand rule** (First finger Field N
→ S, seCond finger Current + → −, thuMb Motion) to find the direction of the force;
explain the combined field around a wire between poles; explain the **moving-coil
loudspeaker** (radial field, alternating current, the cone vibrates); explain the
**turning effect on a coil** and the **moving-coil meter**; describe the simple **d.c.
motor** and the jobs of the **commutator** (split ring: reverses the current every half
turn so the coil keeps turning one way) and the **brushes** (carbon contacts that keep
the spinning coil connected); state four ways to increase a motor's turning effect and two
ways to reverse it; describe the features of a practical motor (several coils, an iron
**armature**, curved pole pieces for a radial field, an electromagnet so it runs on a.c.);
describe **electromagnetic induction** in a wire moved across a field, detected with a
centre-zero **galvanometer**; state **Faraday's law** (induced e.m.f. ∝ rate of cutting
field lines) and the three factors that increase the induced e.m.f.; state what reverses it
and when it is zero; describe induction when a magnet is moved into and out of a coil.

## The deck (24 slides)

Slide 1 is the only unscored slide. `check` ids c1–c15, `activity` ids a1–a8, numbered
consecutively in slide order in the file. (As built: slide 20's check became an `order`
activity, the induction chain, so the deck has three activity types; the ids after it
shift by one from the labels below — c14 → c13, c15 → c14, c16 → c15, a7 → a8.)

1. **hero** — opener. Starter: "A drill, a washing machine, an electric car and the tiny
   buzz in your phone all use the same trick. In one line: what do you think a magnet and
   a current can do to each other?"
2. **callout** `Zap` — Recap. Conventional current + → − · field lines N → S · a coil
   carrying a current is a magnet, with poles at its ends · reversing the current reverses
   its field. **c1** — field lines outside a magnet run… (from N to S / from S to N / in
   circles round the magnet) → N to S.
3. **statement**, Predict — **a1 predict**: a copper wire hangs loosely between the poles
   of a U-shaped magnet. First no current, then the switch is closed. (No force either
   time — copper is non-magnetic / No force until the current flows; then the wire is
   pushed sideways, across the field / Once the current flows the wire is pulled to the N
   pole). correct: pushed across. explain: *copper feels nothing from the magnet, but a
   current makes its own field, and the two fields together push the wire across the gap.*
4. **split** `WIRE_IN_FIELD` — The force on a current. With a current, the wire is pushed
   **upwards** out of the gap. Reverse the current, or turn the magnet round, and it is
   pushed **down**. It always moves *across* the field — never towards a pole. Note (write):
   *A current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field feels a force at right angles to both
   the field and the current.* **c2** — the battery is turned round. (The wire is pushed
   down instead of up / The wire is pulled to the N pole / Nothing changes) → down.
5. **split** `CATAPULT_FIELD` — Why there is a force. The wire's circular field adds to the
   magnet's straight field: on one side of the wire the two agree and the lines bunch up; on
   the other they cancel and the lines thin out. Field lines "try" to straighten, so the
   wire is catapulted from the crowded side towards the thin side. **c3** — the force on the
   wire points… (away from the side where the field lines are crowded / towards the N pole /
   along the wire) → away from crowded side.
6. **steps** — Bigger, smaller, none. Force ↑ with more **current**, a **stronger magnet**,
   a **longer** length of wire in the field. Reverse the current *or* the field → force
   reverses. Wire parallel to the field → **no** force. **a2 sort** — bins *Bigger force* /
   *Force reversed* / *No force*; cards: "increase the current" (bigger), "use a stronger
   magnet" (bigger), "put a longer length of wire in the field" (bigger), "swap the battery
   round" (reversed), "swap the magnet's poles" (reversed), "turn the wire so it lies along
   the field lines" (none), "switch the current off" (none). 7 cards.
7. **split** `FLEMING_LHR` — Fleming's left-hand rule. Hold thumb and first two fingers of
   the **left** hand at right angles: **F**irst finger = **F**ield (N → S); se**C**ond
   finger = **C**urrent (conventional); thu**M**b = **M**otion (the force). Note (write):
   the rule. **c4** — which finger shows the current? (The second finger / The first finger
   / The thumb) → second.
8. **split** `LHR_EXAMPLE` — Using the rule. Field drawn **into the page** (× marks),
   current **up the page**. First finger into the page, second finger up… the thumb points
   **left**: the force is to the left. (Worked out: F = I L × B with B into the page and I
   up the page gives a force towards the left; a right hand would give the opposite — the
   usual slip.) **c5** — same field into the page, but the current now runs **down** the
   page. (Force to the right / Force to the left / No force) → right.
9. **callout** `AlertTriangle` — When the rule works. Only when current and field are at
   right angles; at other angles there is a smaller force in a direction that is harder to
   predict; parallel → no force at all. A beam of charged particles (electrons) is a
   current, so it feels the force too. **c6** — a wire runs parallel to the field lines
   with a large current. (No force / A large force / A force along the wire) → none.
10. **statement**, Predict — **a3 predict**: a loudspeaker's coil sits in a magnet's field
    and carries alternating current. What does the coil do? (Moves in and out, in time with
    the current / Spins round / Heats up and glows). correct: in and out.
11. **split** `LOUDSPEAKER` — The moving-coil loudspeaker. A cylindrical magnet gives a
    **radial** ("spoke-like") field at right angles to every part of the coil. The amplifier
    sends **alternating current**: the force on the coil is backwards, forwards,
    backwards… so the coil and the paper **cone** vibrate and push the air — sound waves.
    **c7** — why must the current alternate? (So the force keeps reversing and the cone
    vibrates / To stop the coil heating / To make the magnet stronger) → reversing.
12. **split** `COIL_TURNING` — Turning effect on a coil. A flat coil between the poles,
    seen end-on: the current comes **towards you** along one long side and goes **away from
    you** along the other, so by the left-hand rule one side is pushed up and the other down
    — a turning effect. More turns → bigger turning effect. Note (write): *the two sides of
    the coil carry current in opposite directions, so their forces are opposite, and the
    coil turns.* **c8** — why does the coil turn rather than
    move bodily? (Its two sides carry current in opposite directions, so the forces on them
    are opposite / Only one side is in the field / The coil is heavier on one side) →
    opposite.
13. **split** `Gauge`, no diagram — The moving-coil meter. The pointer is fixed to a coil
    in a magnet's field, held by springs. More current → bigger turning effect → the coil
    turns further against the springs → the pointer moves further along the scale. **c9** —
    the current through the meter is doubled. (The pointer moves further round the scale /
    The pointer swings to zero / The pointer moves the other way) → further.
14. **statement**, Predict — **a4 predict**: a coil in a field turns until it is vertical.
    If the current kept flowing the *same* way, what next? (It would stop vertical and rock
    to rest / It would keep spinning / It would turn back the other way and keep going).
    correct: stop vertical. explain: *past the vertical the forces turn it back; a motor
    needs something to reverse the current at just that moment.*
15. **showcase** `DC_MOTOR` — The simple d.c. motor. A coil of insulated copper wire free to
    turn between the poles; a **commutator** (split ring) fixed to the coil; two carbon
    **brushes** that rub on the commutator and keep the coil connected to the battery.
    caption: *brushes connect; the commutator reverses.* **c10** — what do the brushes do?
    (Keep the spinning coil connected to the battery / Reverse the current every half turn /
    Provide the magnetic field) → connect.
16. **steps** `COMMUTATOR_ACTION` — The commutator's job. 1 Coil horizontal: forces
    furthest apart, biggest turning effect. 2 Coil reaches vertical: the forces can turn it
    no further. 3 Just past vertical the commutator's gaps pass the brushes and the current
    in the coil **reverses**. 4 The forces reverse too, and push the coil on round the same
    way — half a turn at a time. **c11** — why must the current in the coil reverse every
    half turn? (So the forces keep pushing it round the same way instead of turning it back
    / To stop the coil overheating / So the battery lasts longer) → same way.
17. **split** `RotateCcw`, no diagram — Faster, stronger, backwards. Turning effect ↑ with
    more current, a stronger magnet, more turns, a bigger coil area. Reverse the battery
    **or** the magnet's poles → the motor runs the other way. Reverse **both** → no change.
    **a5 sort** — bins *Bigger turning effect* / *Turns the other way* / *No change*; cards:
    "more turns on the coil" (bigger), "a stronger magnet" (bigger), "a larger current"
    (bigger), "a wider coil" (bigger), "swap the battery's terminals" (other way), "swap
    the magnet's poles" (other way), "swap the battery AND the poles" (no change). 7 cards.
18. **split** `PRACTICAL_MOTOR` — Practical motors. Several coils at different angles,
    each with its own pair of commutator segments → smoother, more turning effect; hundreds
    of turns wound on an iron **armature**, which is magnetised and strengthens the field;
    **curved pole pieces** give a radial field, so the turning effect stays near its maximum
    through most of the turn. With an **electromagnet** for the field, the motor can run on
    a.c.: the field reverses with the current, so the turning effect is always the same
    way. **c12** — why curved pole pieces? (They make the field radial, so the turning effect
    stays high for most of the rotation / They make the motor lighter / They stop the coil
    touching the magnet) → radial.
19. **statement**, Predict — **a6 predict**: a wire joined to a centre-zero galvanometer is
    pulled down through the field between two magnets. (The needle flicks to one side while
    the wire is moving / Nothing — there is no battery / The needle moves and stays over).
    correct: flicks while moving.
20. **split** `INDUCED_WIRE` — Electromagnetic induction. Move a wire **across** a field and
    a small **e.m.f.** (voltage) is **induced** in it — **electromagnetic induction**. If
    the wire is part of a complete circuit, the e.m.f. drives a current, shown by a
    **galvanometer** (centre-zero: the pointer goes left or right with the current
    direction). Note (write): *e.m.f. (electromotive force): the voltage a source provides,
    measured in volts. A wire moving through a magnetic field is a source of e.m.f.*
    **a7 order** (as built; the plan first had a check here) — "The wire is pulled down
    through the field. Put the events in order": the wire moves across the field · field
    lines are cut · an e.m.f. is induced in the wire · the complete circuit lets a current
    flow · the galvanometer needle flicks to one side. (5 steps.)
21. **steps** — Faraday's law and the factors. The induced e.m.f. is **proportional to the
    rate at which field lines are cut**. So: move faster → bigger; stronger magnet (lines
    closer) → bigger; more wire in the field (loop it through several times) → bigger.
    Reverse the motion *or* the field → the e.m.f. reverses. Not moving, or moving
    **parallel** to the lines → nothing cut → zero. **a7 sort** — bins *Bigger e.m.f.* /
    *E.m.f. reversed* / *Zero*; cards: "move the wire faster" (bigger), "use a stronger
    magnet" (bigger), "loop the wire through the field three times" (bigger), "move the wire
    up instead of down" (reversed), "turn the magnet round" (reversed), "hold the wire
    still" (zero), "move the wire along the field lines" (zero). 7 cards.
22. **split** `INDUCED_COIL` — A magnet and a coil. Push a bar magnet into a coil: the
    field lines move instead of the wire, but lines are still cut, so an e.m.f. is induced
    and the galvanometer flicks one way. Pull it **out**: the other way. Push the **S** pole
    in instead of the N: the other way. Hold it still: **nothing**. Faster, stronger, more
    turns → bigger. **c14** — the magnet is pushed in, then held still inside the coil. (The
    needle flicks, then returns to zero / The needle stays deflected / The needle flicks the
    other way) → returns to zero.
23. **compare** — The two effects side by side. Left (motor effect, green accent): current
    + field → force / motion — loudspeaker, meter, motor. Right (generator effect, blue
    accent): motion + field → induced e.m.f. / current — guitar pick-ups, playback heads,
    generators (next unit). **c15** — Faraday's law says the induced e.m.f. is proportional
    to… (the rate at which field lines are cut / the strength of the magnet only / the
    resistance of the wire) → rate.
24. **stack** `variant: 'checklist'`, 3 columns — Can you do all six? use Fleming's left
    hand · give the three force factors · explain the commutator and brushes · reverse a
    motor · state Faraday's law · say when the induced e.m.f. is zero. **c16** — a student
    moves a magnet into a coil twice as fast as before. (The induced e.m.f. is larger / The
    induced e.m.f. is the same / No e.m.f. — the magnet must be still) → larger.

## Questions (5, three marks each; fresh situations)

- **sq1** A straight wire carrying a current lies at right angles to the magnetic field
  between two poles and is pushed upwards. State **two** different changes, each of which
  on its own would make the force act downwards, and **one** change that would make the
  force zero. (1 reverse the current / turn the battery round; 1 reverse the field / swap
  the magnet's poles; 1 turn the wire so it lies parallel to the field lines, or switch the
  current off.) suggestedWords: `[['reverse'], ['parallel'], ['right angles']]`.
- **sq2** Explain how a moving-coil loudspeaker turns an alternating current into sound.
  (1 the coil sits in the radial field of the magnet, so a current in it feels a force,
  given by the left-hand rule; 1 because the current alternates, the force on the coil
  keeps reversing, so the coil and the cone vibrate in and out; 1 the vibrating cone pushes
  the air to and fro, making sound waves.) suggestedWords: `[['radial'], ['reverse'], ['vibrate']]`.
- **sq3** In a simple d.c. motor, explain why the coil turns, and what the commutator does
  and why it is needed. (1 the two sides of the coil carry current in opposite directions,
  so by the left-hand rule the forces on them are opposite and there is a turning effect; 1
  the commutator (split ring) reverses the current in the coil every half turn, just as the
  coil passes the vertical; 1 without it the forces would turn the coil back and it would
  stop vertical — with it the forces keep pushing the same way round.) suggestedWords:
  `[['opposite'], ['split ring'], ['vertical']]`.
- **sq4** A toy motor with one coil and a flat-faced magnet turns weakly and jerkily.
  Explain **two** features of a practical motor that give it a stronger, smoother turning
  effect. Mark scheme, three tickable lines: (1 names two features from: several coils at
  different angles each with its own commutator segments / coils wound on an iron armature /
  curved pole pieces; 1 explains the first named feature — e.g. several coils: one is always
  near the best position, so the turning effect is steadier and larger; armature: the iron
  is magnetised and strengthens the field; curved pole pieces: the field is radial, so the
  turning effect stays near its maximum for most of the turn; 1 explains the second named
  feature in the same way.) suggestedWords: `[['armature'], ['radial'], ['segments']]`.
- **sq5** A bar magnet is pushed N pole first into a coil connected to a centre-zero
  galvanometer, held still inside for a moment, then pulled out again. Describe what the
  galvanometer shows at each stage and explain why. (1 pushed in: the needle deflects one
  way — the moving field lines are cut by the coil, inducing an e.m.f. that drives a
  current; 1 held still: the needle returns to zero — no lines are being cut, so no e.m.f.;
  1 pulled out: the needle deflects the other way — the lines are cut in the opposite sense,
  so the induced e.m.f. and current are reversed.) suggestedWords: `[['cut'], ['induced'], ['opposite']]`.

## Source Analysis (3)

1. **MCQ** on `LHR_EXAMPLE`: "The field is into the page and the current is up the page.
   The force on the wire is to the left. A student now reverses the current. Which way is
   the force?" A to the right (correct) · B still to the left · C up the page · D there is
   no force now.
2. **MCQ** on `COMMUTATOR_ACTION`: "In the middle panel the coil is vertical. Why is there
   no turning effect at that instant?" A the two forces are in line with each other through
   the axle, so they cannot turn the coil (correct) · B the current has stopped · C the coil
   is outside the field · D the brushes have worn out.
3. **Written** on `INDUCED_WIRE`: "The left panel shows the wire moved down, the right
   panel shows it moved up. Describe what the galvanometer does in each case and give
   **two** ways to make the deflection larger." (1 moved down: the needle deflects one way;
   moved up: it deflects the opposite way — the direction of the induced e.m.f. depends on
   the direction of motion; 1 move the wire faster; 1 use a stronger magnet, or put more
   wire in the field by looping it through several times.) `imageAlt` describes the poles,
   the field direction, the motion arrows and the needle positions.

## Quiz (10 MCQ, 12 minutes) — key spread 3 · 3 · 2 · 2

1. In Fleming's left-hand rule the first finger shows… → the field, N to S (B)
2. A wire carrying a current lies parallel to the field. The force is… → zero (D)
3. The force on a current in a field acts… → at right angles to both the field and the
   current (A)
4. Which change does NOT increase the force on the wire? → using a longer wire outside the
   field / making the wire thicker with the same current (C) — distractors: more current,
   stronger magnet, more wire in the field
5. The two sides of a coil in a field are pushed in opposite directions because… → the
   current flows in opposite directions along them (A)
6. The job of the brushes is… → to keep the turning coil connected to the supply (B)
7. The commutator… → reverses the current in the coil every half turn (C)
8. A motor is running clockwise. The battery is reversed AND the magnet is turned round.
   → it still runs clockwise (D)
9. Faraday's law: the induced e.m.f. is proportional to… → the rate at which field lines
   are cut (A)
10. A magnet is held still inside a coil. The galvanometer reads… → zero, because no
    field lines are being cut (B)
