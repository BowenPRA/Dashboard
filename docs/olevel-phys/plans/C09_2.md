# Currents, Coils and Electromagnets — unit plan (OLEVEL_PHYS / C09_2)

Chapter 9, spreads **9.3 Magnetic effect of a current** (pages 202–203) and **9.4
Electromagnets** (pages 204–205). A **rules and devices** unit: a current makes a field,
two right-hand grip rules say which way it points, and then the machines that use it.
English-only (`bilingual: false`).

Recaps `C09_1` in one slide. Stops before 9.5 (the force on a current).

## Objective

The student can state that a current produces a magnetic field; describe the field around
a straight wire (circles, strongest close to the wire, stronger with more current); use
**conventional current** (+ to −); apply the **right-hand grip rule** for the field
direction round a wire; describe the field of a single-turn **coil** and of a **solenoid**
(like a bar magnet, poles at the ends); state the two ways to strengthen a coil's field
(more current, more turns) and that reversing the current reverses the field; apply the
grip rule for a coil's poles (fingers with the current, thumb to N); describe magnetising
a steel bar in a solenoid with **direct current** and demagnetising a magnet by drawing it
out of a solenoid carrying **alternating current**; define an **electromagnet** (coil +
soft **core**), explain why the core is iron or Mumetal and not steel, and state the
strength factors; explain the **relay** (input circuit, electromagnet, iron **armature**,
**contacts**, output circuit; normally open / normally closed) and why a small switch can
control a large current; explain the **circuit breaker** (electromagnet, iron catch,
contacts, **reset**) and its advantage over a fuse; describe **magnetic recording** (tape
or disc coated with iron oxide, a recording head that is a tiny electromagnet).

## The deck (24 slides)

Slide 1 is the only unscored slide. `check` ids c1–c16, `activity` ids a1–a7.

1. **hero** — opener. Starter: "A scrapyard crane lifts a whole car with a magnet, swings
   it over the pile — and lets go. A permanent magnet cannot let go. In one sentence, what
   must be different about this magnet?"
2. **callout** `Magnet` — Recap from the last unit. Like poles repel, unlike attract ·
   field lines run N → S and show the force on an N pole · soft iron loses its magnetism at
   once, steel keeps it. **c1** — which material loses its magnetism as soon as the
   magnetising field is removed? (Soft iron / Steel / Alcomax) → soft iron.
3. **statement**, Predict — **a1 predict**: a plotting compass sits beside a straight wire.
   The switch is closed and a current flows. (The needle turns / Nothing — a wire is not a
   magnet / The needle points along the wire). correct: turns. explain: *the current makes a
   magnetic field around the wire, and the needle lines up with it.*
4. **split** `WIRE_FIELD` — The field around a wire. Features: the field lines are
   **circles** centred on the wire; the field is **strongest close to the wire**;
   **increasing the current increases the strength**. Note (write): those three. **c2** —
   which is the correct description? (Circles round the wire, closer together near the wire
   / Straight lines pointing away from the wire / The same pattern as a bar magnet) →
   circles.
5. **split** `Zap`, no diagram — Conventional current. In a circuit the current is a flow
   of electrons, but the arrows on circuit diagrams run from + to −: the **conventional
   current direction**. Electrons, being negative, flow the other way. Every rule in this
   chapter uses the conventional direction. **c3** — a battery's + terminal is on the left.
   Which way do the circuit's current arrows point? (From the + on the left round to the −
   / From the − round to the + / Both ways at once) → from +.
6. **split** `GRIP_RULE_WIRE` — The right-hand grip rule for a wire. Grip the wire with the
   **right** hand, **thumb** along the conventional current; the **fingers** curl in the
   direction of the field lines. Note (write): the rule. **c4** — the current in the wire
   is reversed. What happens to the field? (It points the opposite way round the circles /
   It disappears / It becomes stronger) → opposite way.
7. **split** `COIL_FIELD` — One turn of wire. Bend the wire into a loop: the field from
   every part of the wire adds up inside the loop, so the field there is stronger than
   round a straight wire. **c5** — why is the field inside a loop stronger than beside a
   straight wire? (The fields from all parts of the wire add together inside the loop / The
   wire is shorter / The current is bigger in a loop) → add together.
8. **split** `SOLENOID_FIELD` — The solenoid. A long coil of many turns is a **solenoid**.
   Its field is like a **bar magnet's**: lines run out of one end, round and in at the other,
   with magnetic poles at the ends. Note (write): *Solenoid: a long coil of wire; its field
   is the same shape as a bar magnet's, with a pole at each end.* **c6** — where are a
   solenoid's poles? (At its two ends / Along its sides / It has no poles, only circles) →
   ends.
9. **steps** — Changing the field of a coil. 1 More **current** → stronger. 2 More
   **turns** → stronger. 3 Reverse the current → the field reverses (the poles swap). 4 A
   soft iron core (coming up) → much stronger. **a2 sort** — bins *Stronger* / *Weaker* /
   *Reversed*; cards: "double the current" (stronger), "add more turns" (stronger), "halve
   the current" (weaker), "remove half the turns" (weaker), "swap the battery round"
   (reversed), "connect the + to the other end of the coil" (reversed). 6 cards.
10. **split** `GRIP_RULE_COIL` — The grip rule for a coil's poles. Grip the coil with the
    right hand so the **fingers** follow the conventional current round the turns; the
    **thumb** points to the coil's **N pole**. Note (write): the rule. **c7** — the battery
    on a solenoid is turned round. (The N and S poles swap ends / The field gets weaker /
    Nothing changes — the coil is the same) → swap.
11. **split** `SOLENOID_POLES_Q` — Which end is N? A solenoid drawn with the current's
    direction shown on the front of its turns (the battery + on the right, so the current
    on the near side of each turn runs **downwards**; worked out: fingers curling down at
    the front and up at the back is anticlockwise seen from the right-hand end, so the
    **N pole is the right-hand end**). Content: talk the student through the grip.
    **a3 hotspot** on `SOLENOID_POLES_Q` — "Tap the end of the solenoid that is the N
    pole." correct: the right-hand end; decoys: the left-hand end, the middle of the coil,
    the battery. *Draw the diagram so this is unambiguous and re-derive it before you write
    the answer; if your drawing makes the left end N, set the answer to the left end and
    say so in the report.*
12. **split** `MAKE_MAGNET_SOLENOID` — Making a magnet. A steel bar inside a solenoid; a
    large **direct current (d.c.)** — one way only — magnetises it and makes the field much
    stronger; switch off and the **steel stays magnetised**. Nearly all permanent magnets
    are made this way. **c8** — why is steel used, not iron? (Steel keeps its magnetism when
    the current stops; iron would lose it / Steel is a better conductor / Iron cannot be
    magnetised) → keeps magnetism.
13. **split** `Repeat` icon, no diagram or a small two-wave sketch — Demagnetising with a
    coil. **Alternating current (a.c.)** flows backwards, forwards, backwards… so the
    solenoid's field flips direction rapidly. Pull a magnet slowly out of it and the atomic
    magnets are thrown out of line: demagnetised. Note (write): *d.c. flows one way; a.c.
    reverses direction many times a second.* **c9** — why must the current be a.c.? (Its
    field keeps reversing, which throws the atomic magnets out of line / a.c. is stronger /
    a.c. heats the magnet) → reversing.
14. **statement**, Predict — **a4 predict**: a bar of soft iron is slid into the middle of
    a current-carrying coil. The field… (becomes about a thousand times stronger / stays
    about the same / becomes weaker, because iron blocks it). correct: thousand times.
15. **split** `ELECTROMAGNET` — The electromagnet. A **coil** of several hundred turns of
    insulated copper wire round a **core** of iron or Mumetal. The current magnetises the
    core: about a thousand times stronger than the coil alone. Core magnetism is
    **temporary** — lost as soon as the current is switched off. Note (write):
    *Electromagnet: a coil with a soft iron core; a magnet that can be switched on and off.*
    **c10** — a steel core would be a mistake because… (it would stay magnetised when
    switched off / it would melt / it is non-magnetic) → stay magnetised.
16. **compare** — Permanent magnet vs electromagnet. Left: always on; fixed strength;
    poles fixed. Right: on/off with a switch; strength set by current and turns; poles
    reversed by reversing the current. **c11** — which can be switched off? (Only the
    electromagnet / Only the permanent magnet / Both) → electromagnet.
17. **split** `Gauge` icon, no diagram — Making it stronger. Strength ↑ with more current,
    more turns (and the soft core). Reversing the current reverses the field. **c12** — a
    student cannot change the power supply but wants a stronger electromagnet. (Wind more
    turns on the coil / Use thinner insulation / Replace the iron core with steel) → more
    turns.
18. **statement**, Predict — **a5 predict**: a car's starter motor needs a current far too
    big for the thin wires behind the dashboard, yet a small key switch starts it. How?
    (The key switch works an electromagnet that closes a second, heavy-duty switch / The
    key switch carries the big current for a moment / The motor starts itself). correct:
    electromagnet.
19. **split** `RELAY`, `ratio: 40` — The relay. A switch operated by an electromagnet. Input
    circuit: switch S, a battery, the electromagnet. Output circuit: the motor and its own
    power supply, joined through **contacts C**. Between them, a pivoted **iron armature**.
    **a6 order** — "Put the events in order when S is closed": 1 a small current flows in
    the electromagnet's coil · 2 the iron core becomes magnetised · 3 the iron armature is
    pulled towards it · 4 the armature pushes the contacts C together · 5 a large current
    flows in the motor circuit · 6 the motor runs. (6 steps.)
20. **split** `Split` icon, no diagram — Normally open, normally closed. The relay on the
    last slide is **normally open**: input OFF → output OFF. A **normally closed** relay is
    the other way round: input ON → output OFF. Most relays can be wired either way. Why
    use one: the small switch and thin wires carry only the tiny input current; the thick
    wires of the output circuit carry the big one. **c14** — in a normally closed relay, the
    input switch is turned ON. (The output circuit is switched OFF / The output circuit is
    switched ON / Both circuits are off) → OFF. *(No c13: the order activity took slide 19.)*
21. **split** `CIRCUIT_BREAKER` — The circuit breaker. An automatic switch that cuts off the
    current if it rises above a set value — the same job as a fuse, but it can be **reset**
    after it has **tripped**. The current flows through the contacts and through an
    electromagnet. Note (write): *Circuit breaker: an automatic switch that trips if the
    current is too high, and can be reset.* **c15** — advantage over a fuse? (It can be
    reset instead of replaced / It lets a bigger current through / It works without
    electricity) → reset.
22. **steps** — How it trips. **a7 order** — "Put the events in order when a fault makes
    the current too high": 1 the current in the electromagnet rises · 2 the electromagnet's
    pull becomes strong enough · 3 the iron catch is released · 4 the spring pulls the
    contacts apart · 5 the current stops · 6 the reset button is pressed to close the
    contacts again. (6 steps.)
23. **split** `RECORDING_HEAD` — Magnetic recording. Tape or a hard-disc surface is coated
    with **iron oxide**, magnetically between soft and hard: it keeps a pattern but can be
    re-recorded. The **recording head** is a tiny electromagnet whose current is varied by
    the sound; it leaves a track of varying magnetism along the tape or disc. **c16** — why
    is iron oxide a good coating? (It keeps its magnetism, but can still be demagnetised for
    the next recording / It is a soft magnetic material that loses the pattern at once / It
    is non-magnetic) → keeps but re-recordable.
24. **stack** `variant: 'checklist'`, 3 columns — Can you do all six? draw the field round a
    wire · use the grip rule for a wire · use the grip rule for a coil · say what a core is
    for · explain a relay · explain a circuit breaker. **c16** — a solenoid's N pole is at
    its left end. The current is reversed and the number of turns doubled. (N is now at the right end, and
    the field is stronger / N stays at the left end, and the field is stronger / N is at the
    right end, and the field is weaker) → right, stronger.

## Questions (5, three marks each; fresh situations)

- **sq1** Describe the magnetic field around a long straight wire carrying a current, and
  state how its strength depends on distance from the wire and on the current. (1 the
  field lines are circles centred on the wire, in planes at right angles to it; 1 weaker
  further from the wire — the circles are further apart; 1 stronger when the current is
  increased.) suggestedWords: `[['pattern'], ['distance'], ['increase']]`.
- **sq2** A solenoid is connected to a battery. State the rule for finding which end is its
  N pole, and explain what happens to its poles if the battery is connected the other way
  round. (1 grip the coil with the right hand with the fingers pointing the way the
  conventional current flows round the turns; 1 the thumb then points to the N pole; 1
  reversing the battery reverses the current, so the field reverses and the N and S poles
  swap ends.) suggestedWords: `[['right hand'], ['conventional'], ['reverse']]`.
- **sq3** An electromagnet is made by winding a coil around a bar of iron. Explain what
  the iron bar is for, why iron is used rather than steel, and give one other way to make
  the electromagnet stronger. (1 the core is magnetised by the coil's field and makes the
  field about a thousand times stronger; 1 iron is a soft magnetic material: it loses its
  magnetism as soon as the current stops, whereas steel would stay magnetised; 1 increase
  the current or increase the number of turns.) suggestedWords: `[['core'], ['temporary'], ['turns']]`.
- **sq4** The heater of a greenhouse takes a large current, but the gardener switches it
  on with a small, thin-wired switch by the door. Explain, using the word *relay*, how
  this works. (1 the small switch closes the input circuit so a small current flows through
  the relay's electromagnet; 1 the magnetised core attracts the iron armature, which closes
  the contacts; 1 the contacts complete the separate output circuit, whose thick wires carry
  the heater's large current — the small switch never carries it.) suggestedWords:
  `[['input', 'output'], ['armature'], ['contacts']]`.
- **sq5** A circuit breaker protects the wiring of a kitchen. Explain how it switches the
  current off when a fault makes the current too large, and state one advantage it has over
  a fuse. (1 the current flows through the breaker's electromagnet, so a larger current makes
  a stronger pull; 1 when the pull is strong enough it releases the iron catch and the
  contacts spring open, stopping the current; 1 it can be reset with the button — a fuse has
  to be replaced.) suggestedWords: `[['electromagnet'], ['catch'], ['reset']]`.

## Source Analysis (3)

1. **MCQ** on `WIRE_FIELD`: "The current is reversed, so it now flows into the page. Which
   describes the new field?" A the same circles, but clockwise (correct) · B the same
   circles, still anticlockwise · C straight lines pointing away from the wire · D no field,
   because the current is going the other way.

   running. What happens, and why?" A the motor stops: the electromagnet switches off, the
   armature springs back and contacts C open (correct) · B the motor keeps running because
   its own supply is unbroken · C the motor speeds up · D the input battery now powers the
   motor.

   the current rises above its limit, and how it is put back into use." (1 the larger
   current makes the electromagnet pull harder on the iron catch; 1 the catch is released,
   the spring pulls the contacts apart and the current stops; 1 the reset button pushes the
   contacts closed again, so nothing needs replacing.) `imageAlt` describes every labelled
