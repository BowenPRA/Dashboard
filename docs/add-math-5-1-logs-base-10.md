# Add Maths 5.1 — Logarithms to base 10

Source section: Cambridge IGCSE and O Level Additional Mathematics coursebook (3rd
edition), section 5.1, pages 100–103. Read in the Cambridge GO reader on 2026-09-30.

Three parts: the beats of the notes in our own words, a map of the exercise's question
types, and a fresh problem set that covers every type, with answers. None of the
problems are the book's. Every answer was checked by script.

## Part 1 — The beats of the notes

1. **A problem powers cannot finish (p. 100).** Solve 10ˣ = 30. It sits between 10¹ and
   10², so x is between 1 and 2, but index work alone cannot pin it down.
2. **Estimate from a graph.** Read across and down on the curve y = 10ˣ to get roughly
   1.48.
3. **Name the function.** The exact answer is written log₁₀ 30, read "log 30 to base
   10". "Log" is short for logarithm. It is also written lg 30 or log 30.
4. **The calculator key (p. 101).** Use the log or lg key for base 10.
5. **The rule, twice.** In symbols: if y = 10ˣ then x = log₁₀ y. In words: the log is
   the power that 10 must be raised to. A one-line example follows.
6. **Inverse functions.** y = 10ˣ and y = lg x are reflections of each other in y = x,
   shown on one diagram.
7. **Four facts to explain (class discussion).** lg 10 = 1, lg 1 = 0, lg 10ˣ = x for any
   x, and 10^(lg x) = x for positive x. Students say why each is true.
8. **Worked example 1.** Exponential form to log form, by two methods: a three-step
   "identify base and index, write the frame, fill in" routine, and taking logs of both
   sides. Then solve to 3 s.f.
9. **Worked example 2 (p. 102).** Log form to exponential form by the same two methods,
   then solve to 3 s.f.
10. **Worked example 3 (pp. 102–103).** Evaluate without a calculator by writing the
    number as a power of 10: a whole power, a decimal, and a product with a surd.

## Part 2 — What the exercise asks

Exercise 5.1 is on page 103: five questions of six parts each.

| Type | Book question | What it asks | How it climbs |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Exponential form to log form | 1 | Rewrite the statement | Known powers of 10 first, then an unknown power x |
| B. Solve 10ˣ = k | 2 | Answer to 3 s.f. | Numbers above 1, then decimals below 1 (negative answers) |
| C. Log form to exponential form | 3 | Rewrite the statement | Numerical facts first, then lg x = a decimal, including a negative |
| D. Solve lg x = k | 4 | Answer to 3 s.f. | Positive k, then negative k |
| E. Evaluate without a calculator | 5 | Write as a power of 10 | Whole power, decimal, roots, then products and quotients of powers and roots |

## Part 3 — Fresh problems in the same style

### A. Exponential form to log form

Write in logarithmic form.

- A1. 10⁴ = 10 000
- A2. 10⁰ = 1
- A3. 10⁻² = 0.01
- A4. 10⁷ = 10 000 000
- A5. 10ˣ = 7
- A6. 10ˣ = 250
- A7. 10ˣ = 0.3
- A8. 10²ˣ = 9

### B. Solve 10ˣ = k

Solve, giving your answer correct to 3 significant figures.

- B1. 10ˣ = 52
- B2. 10ˣ = 8
- B3. 10ˣ = 430
- B4. 10ˣ = 2750
- B5. 10ˣ = 1.5
- B6. 10ˣ = 0.4
- B7. 10ˣ = 0.063
- B8. 10ˣ = 0.0009

### C. Log form to exponential form

Write in exponential form.

- C1. lg 1000 = 3
- C2. lg 0.1 = −1
- C3. lg 1 = 0
- C4. lg (1/100) = −2
- C5. lg x = 3.5
- C6. lg x = 0.6
- C7. lg x = −1.2

### D. Solve lg x = k

Solve, giving your answer correct to 3 significant figures.

- D1. lg x = 1.3
- D2. lg x = 2.75
- D3. lg x = 0.48
- D4. lg x = 4.2
- D5. lg x = −0.4
- D6. lg x = −1.1
- D7. lg x = −2.5

### E. Evaluate without a calculator

Find the value of each of these.

- E1. lg 1 000 000
- E2. lg 0.0001
- E3. lg √1000
- E4. lg ⁴√10 (the fourth root of 10)
- E5. lg (100 ∛10)
- E6. lg (10 ÷ √10)
- E7. lg (1 ÷ (10√10))
- E8. lg (√10 × ∛10)

### F. Stretch (not in the book's exercise)

- F1. Without a calculator, say which two consecutive whole numbers lg 4500 lies
  between. Do the same for lg 0.02.
- F2. A whole number N has lg N = 6.2. How many digits does N have?
- F3. Solve 10²ˣ = 50, correct to 3 s.f.
- F4. Solve 10ˣ⁺¹ = 7, correct to 3 s.f.
- F5. Solve lg (2x − 1) = 1.5, correct to 3 s.f.
- F6. Solve lg (lg x) = 0.
- F7. The pH of a solution is −lg c, where c is the hydrogen ion concentration.
  1. Find the pH when c = 0.00025.
  2. Find c when the pH is 8.3.
- F8. Explain why lg (−5) has no value, and why lg 0 has no value.

## Answers

**A.** A1 lg 10 000 = 4. A2 lg 1 = 0. A3 lg 0.01 = −2. A4 lg 10 000 000 = 7.
A5 x = lg 7. A6 x = lg 250. A7 x = lg 0.3. A8 2x = lg 9.

**B.** B1 1.72. B2 0.903. B3 2.63. B4 3.44. B5 0.176. B6 −0.398. B7 −1.20. B8 −3.05.

**C.** C1 10³ = 1000. C2 10⁻¹ = 0.1. C3 10⁰ = 1. C4 10⁻² = 1/100. C5 x = 10^3.5.
C6 x = 10^0.6. C7 x = 10^−1.2.

**D.** D1 20.0. D2 562. D3 3.02. D4 15 800. D5 0.398. D6 0.0794. D7 0.00316.

**E.** E1 6. E2 −4. E3 3/2. E4 1/4. E5 7/3. E6 1/2. E7 −3/2. E8 5/6.

**F.** F1 Between 3 and 4, because 1000 < 4500 < 10 000. Between −2 and −1, because
0.01 < 0.02 < 0.1. F2 Seven digits: N is between 10⁶ and 10⁷. F3 x = ½ lg 50 = 0.849.
F4 x = lg 7 − 1 = −0.155. F5 2x − 1 = 10^1.5, so x = 16.3. F6 lg x = 1, so x = 10.
F7 pH 3.60; c = 10^−8.3 = 5.01 × 10⁻⁹. F8 No power of 10 is negative or zero: 10ˣ is
positive for every x.
