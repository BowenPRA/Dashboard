# Add Maths 5.3 — The laws of logarithms

Source section: Cambridge IGCSE and O Level Additional Mathematics coursebook (3rd
edition), section 5.3, pages 106–108. Read in the Cambridge GO reader on 2026-09-30.

Three parts: the beats of the notes in our own words, a map of the exercise's question
types, and a fresh problem set that covers every type, with answers. None of the
problems are the book's. Every answer was checked by script.

## Part 1 — The beats of the notes

1. **The three laws (p. 106).** For positive x and y and a valid base a:
   - multiplication law: logₐ (xy) = logₐ x + logₐ y
   - division law: logₐ (x/y) = logₐ x − logₐ y
   - power law: logₐ xᵐ = m logₐ x
2. **Proofs.** Each law is proved in three or four lines by writing x and y as powers of
   a and using the index laws.
3. **One more rule (p. 107).** From the power law, logₐ (1/x) = −logₐ x.
4. **Worked example 7.** Combine into a single log: a sum, a difference, and a sum with
   coefficients in front (use the power law first).
5. **Worked example 8.** Given two logs named x and y, express other logs in terms of x
   and y, including one where a whole number comes out because the number equals the
   base.

**Misprint to avoid:** the second part of worked example 7 prints a ÷ between two logs
where a − is meant. Dividing two logs is not the division law. Never copy it.

## Part 2 — What the exercise asks

Exercise 5.3 is on pages 107–108: nine questions.

| Type | Book question | What it asks | How it climbs |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Write as a single log | 1 (nine parts) | Combine terms | Two terms, then coefficients, fractional coefficients, then a whole number added to or taken from a log |
| B. Single log, then simplify | 2 (six parts) | Combine, then evaluate | The combined log is a power of the base |
| C. Mixed simplify | 3 (two parts) | Three terms, one may be a whole number | Coefficients and a constant together |
| D. A log divided by a log | 4, 5 | Write both numbers as powers of the same number, then cancel | Question 4 is guided; question 5 is four to do |
| E. In terms of u | 6 (four parts) | Given u = log x, rewrite | Includes writing x itself as a power |
| F. In terms of x and y | 7 (four parts) | Given two logs, rewrite | Includes writing a product as a power of the base |
| G. Find the value | 8, 9 (four parts each) | Given two log values, evaluate | Reciprocals, roots, products, mixed powers |

### H. Stretch (not in the book's exercise)

- H1. Given that lg 2 ≈ 0.301 and lg 3 ≈ 0.477, find without a calculator: lg 6, lg 8,
  lg 5, lg 1.5, lg 12, lg √3 and lg 0.2.
- H2. Given that logₐ 3 = p and logₐ 5 = q, express in terms of p and q: logₐ 45,
  logₐ 0.6 and logₐ (75a).
- H3. True or false? Give a reason or a counter-example.
  1. log (x + y) = log x + log y
  2. log x² = 2 log x, for x > 0
  3. (log x)² = 2 log x
  4. logₐ (1/x) = −logₐ x
  5. log x ÷ log y = log (x − y)
- H4. Show that lg 2 + lg 5 = 1. Hence find the exact value of
  (lg 5)² + lg 2 × lg 5 + lg 2.
- H5. Find the exact value of
  log₂ (1 + 1/1) + log₂ (1 + 1/2) + log₂ (1 + 1/3) + … + log₂ (1 + 1/7).

## Answers

**A.** A1 log₃ 28. A2 log₅ 6. A3 log₂ 75. A4 log₆ 2. A5 log₇ 30. A6 log₂ (1/3).
A7 log₅ 20. A8 lg 0.7. A9 log₃ (9/5). A10 logₐ (xy³/√z).

**B.** B1 log₃ 27 = 3. B2 log₄ 16 = 2. B3 log₆ 36 = 2. B4 log₅ 5 = 1. B5 log₂ 16 = 4.
B6 log₆ 216 = 3. B7 log₃ (1/9) = −2.

**C.** C1 lg 100 = 2. C2 log₂ 1 = 0. C3 log₅ 125 = 3.

**D.** D1 32 = 2⁵ and 0.125 = 2⁻³, so the ratio is 5 log₅ 2 ÷ (−3 log₅ 2) = −5/3.
D2 2. D3 3. D4 −3. D5 4/3. D6 1/6. D7 The division law turns a *difference* of logs
into the log of a quotient. It says nothing about a quotient of logs.
log₃ 12 − log₃ 4 = 1, but log₃ 12 ÷ log₃ 4 = 1.79.

**E.** E1 3ᵘ. E2 2 + u. E3 u − 3. E4 u/2. E5 1 − 2u. E6 5u/2 − 4.

**F.** F1 x + y. F2 3 + x. F3 3x − y. F4 x/2 − 2 − y. F5 −x − 2y. F6 2^(x + y).
F7 2^(x − 2y).

**G.** G1 15. G2 3. G3 −6. G4 3. G5 6. G6 19. G7 14.

**H.** H1 0.778, 0.903, 0.699 (lg 10 − lg 2), 0.176, 1.079, 0.2385, −0.699.
H2 2p + q; p − q; p + 2q + 1. H3 False (try x = y = 1 in base 10: lg 2 is not 0);
true (power law); false (try x = 10: 1 is not 2); true; false (the law is for a
difference of logs, not a quotient). H4 lg 2 + lg 5 = lg 10 = 1. The expression is
lg 5 (lg 5 + lg 2) + lg 2 = lg 5 + lg 2 = 1. H5 The terms are log₂ (2/1), log₂ (3/2),
…, log₂ (8/7). Their sum is log₂ 8 = 3.
